## Q61

**Answer:** **facile**

**Reasoning:** The context suggests that the contestant’s proposal for world peace was naïve and oversimplified: she blithely urged everyone to “love one another,” implying that peace could be achieved without grappling with complex political realities.  **Facile** means “easily accomplished or attained” and often carries the negative connotation of being *superficial or simplistic*【427385674871878†L91-L100】.  Describing her idea as facile captures its shallow, overly simple nature.  The other adjectives either denote complexity or virtues that are not implied by the passage.

| Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|
| **convoluted** | Highly complex or intricate; would imply her proposal was overly complicated, the opposite of the simple suggestion given. |
| **facile** | Too easily accomplished; suggests superficiality and shallow thinking【427385674871878†L91-L100】.  Accurately describes the contestant’s simplistic solution. **Correct.** |
| **impeccable** | Flawless or perfect; nothing in the sentence suggests her proposal was perfect. |
| **amicable** | Friendly and good‑natured; this does not describe an idea. |
| **soothing** | Calming; again, not relevant to the quality of the idea. |

## Q62

**Answer:** **exemplifies**

**Reasoning:** Community‑based participatory research treats tribal nations and universities as equal partners.  The collaboration described follows this model: tribal citizens worked alongside scientists and continue to help collect data.  Thus, it **exemplifies** (serves as an example of) the approach.  The other verbs either imply avoidance (circumvent), outshining (eclipses), invention (fabricates), or harm (undermines) and do not fit.

| Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|
| **circumvent** | To avoid or go around; the collaboration does not avoid the model. |
| **eclipses** | Outshines or surpasses; the sentence describes following a model, not surpassing it. |
| **fabricates** | Makes up or invents; not relevant. |
| **exemplifies** | Serves as a typical example; accurately describes the collaboration’s adherence to the model. **Correct.** |
| **undermines** | Weakens; the collaboration strengthens, rather than weakens, community participation. |

## Q63

**Answer:** **(i) an appealing shyness; (ii) enthralled**

**Reasoning:** Jimmy Stewart was known for his hesitant, understated delivery.  His hesitancy is described as an **appealing shyness**—a modest demeanor that audiences found endearing.  Despite this understatement, he captivated his audience; to **enthrall** means to hold spellbound or to charm【328020999013979†L92-L120】.  Thus, he spoke with an appealing shyness that enthralled audiences.  The other options either mischaracterize his style (colorful fervor or overwhelming passion) or suggest an adverse audience reaction.

| Slot | Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blank (i)** | **a colorful fervor** | Implies flamboyant passion, inconsistent with Stewart’s hesitancy. |
|  | **an overwhelming passion** | Connotes intensity; again, contrary to his understated style. |
|  | **an appealing shyness** | A modest, endearing reserve; matches the description of hesitancy and understatement. **Correct.** |
| **Blank (ii)** | **enthralled** | To hold spellbound or charm【328020999013979†L92-L120】; audiences were captivated by his performance. **Correct.** |
|  | **repelled** | Driven away; the passage says he was captivating, not off‑putting. |
|  | **amused** | Mildly entertained; “captivating” implies a stronger effect than mere amusement. |

## Q64

**Answer:** **enfeeble**

**Reasoning:** Although grand urban train stations are celebrated, the sentence notes that they enable migration to the suburbs, draining the city’s population and weakening the urban core.  **Enfeeble** means to make weak or deprive of strength【874342713277291†L88-L90】.  The stations therefore enfeeble the city rather than invigorate (strengthen), enlarge (grow), delineate (outline), or simply “overshadow” it.

| Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|
| **invigorate** | To give strength or energy; the stations do the opposite. |
| **enlarge** | To make bigger; building train stations does not make the city bigger in this context. |
| **enfeeble** | To weaken or deprive of strength【874342713277291†L88-L90】; matches the idea that stations contribute to urban decline. **Correct.** |
| **delineate** | To describe or portray; not relevant. |
| **overshadow** | To make someone or something less important; while train stations may dominate the skyline, the key effect described is weakening, not mere distraction. |

## Q65

**Answer:** **(i) successful; (ii) frequency of; (iii) deceptive**

**Reasoning:** According to the company’s own statistics, its safety efforts appeared **successful** because incidents declined; the data showed that the **frequency of** incidents with potential for serious accidents dropped.  However, independent analysts argue the statistics are **deceptive**—the company underestimated both probability and severity of accidents.  The other choices mischaracterize the situation: “innovative” emphasizes novelty rather than efficacy; “frustrated” implies failure; “impediments to” and “attention to” do not collocate naturally with “incidents”; “verifiable” and “consistent” contradict the analysts’ claim that the data are misleading.

| Slot | Option | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blank (i)** | **innovative** | Highlights new methods but not necessarily effective results. |
|  | **successful** | Indicates the safety measures seemed effective, matching the company’s claim. **Correct.** |
|  | **frustrated** | Means thwarted; inappropriate because the company claims improvement. |
| **Blank (ii)** | **frequency of** | Refers to how often incidents occur; the data showed this rate declined. **Correct.** |
|  | **impediments to** | Would mean obstacles to incidents, an awkward phrasing. |
|  | **attention to** | Focus or consideration; the company measured incidents themselves, not attention to them. |
| **Blank (iii)** | **deceptive** | Misleading; aligns with analysts’ criticism of the company’s statistics. **Correct.** |
|  | **verifiable** | Capable of being proven true; analysts suggest the opposite. |
|  | **consistent** | Suggests the data are reliable; again opposite to “deceptive.” |

## Q66

**Answer:** **civil**

**Reasoning:** The sentence contrasts today’s “highly partisan political environment” with a time when ideological opponents could set aside differences and cooperate.  In that earlier era, political discourse was **civil**—polite and courteous.  The Cambridge dictionary defines the *polite* sense of civil as *“polite and formal”*【382805931264953†L163-L168】.  None of the other adjectives describe cordial or respectful discourse.

| Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|
| **civil** | Polite and formal【382805931264953†L163-L168】; describes constructive, respectful political cooperation. **Correct.** |
| **immutable** | Unchangeable; irrelevant here. |
| **polemical** | Involving strong arguments or controversy; this describes today’s environment, not the better past. |
| **efficacious** | Effective; does not describe interpersonal tone. |
| **adequate** | Sufficient; again not about civility. |

## Q67

**Answer:** **minutiae**

**Reasoning:** Lincoln believed that McClellan became bogged down in minor logistical details at the expense of strategic goals.  **Minutiae** are *minute or minor details*【910776770694113†L81-L83】.  The other options mischaracterize what he focused on: “irrelevancies” are unimportant items (stronger than “minute details”); “tactics” and “strategy” describe levels of military planning; “wrongdoing” refers to misconduct.

| Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|
| **irrelevancies** | Things that are irrelevant; while McClellan’s focus may have been misplaced, the text emphasizes small details, not outright irrelevance. |
| **tactics** | Specific maneuvers in battle; he was not solely concerned with battle tactics but trivial details. |
| **minutiae** | Minor details or small points【910776770694113†L81-L83】; matches the passage. **Correct.** |
| **strategy** | Overall planning; Lincoln’s complaint is that he ignored strategy. |
| **wrongdoing** | Misconduct; not indicated. |

## Q68

**Answer:** **(i) menacing; (ii) unprovoked**

**Reasoning:** The Tasmanian devil is not regarded as **menacing** because it rarely attacks humans without provocation.  Most encounters occur when the animal is cornered or feels threatened; only a few are **unprovoked**.  Thus, the devil is not considered dangerous.  “Adverse” means unfavorable and “unpredictable” means random—neither directly refers to menace.  Likewise, “premature” and “impractical” do not describe confrontations.

| Slot | Option | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blank (i)** | **adverse** | Means harmful or unfavorable; while the devil may cause harm, the key issue is menace (threat). |
|  | **menacing** | Threatening; the statement says the animal is not considered menacing. **Correct for negation.** |
|  | **unpredictable** | Suggesting randomness; the issue is provocation, not predictability. |
| **Blank (ii)** | **premature** | Happening before the usual time; irrelevant. |
|  | **impractical** | Not practical; unrelated. |
|  | **unprovoked** | Occurring without a trigger; only a few confrontations were unprovoked, so the animal is not seen as menacing. **Correct.** |

## Q69

**Answer:** **(i) mask; (ii) part and parcel of**

**Reasoning:** Service workers often **mask** their true feelings—hide frustration or sadness—so they can smile at customers.  This emotional labor is **part and parcel of** the job, meaning it is an inherent or essential component.  The other options either don’t capture the idea of concealment or mischaracterize the necessity of the behavior.

| Slot | Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blank (i)** | **masquerade** | Pretend to be something one is not; stronger than simply concealing emotions. |
|  | **simulate** | To imitate; doesn’t necessarily imply hiding one’s actual feelings. |
|  | **mask** | To conceal or hide; describes hiding true emotions behind a smile. **Correct.** |
| **Blank (ii)** | **the apex of** | The highest point; not appropriate. |
|  | **disjoined from** | Separated; also wrong. |
|  | **part and parcel of** | An essential or inherent part; smiling through emotional labor is intrinsic to service work. **Correct.** |

## Q70

**Answer:** **(i) diurnal; (ii) gregarious; (iii) camaraderie**

**Reasoning:** Vervet monkeys are active during the day, so they are **diurnal**—biologically “active chiefly in the daytime”【473955585669182†L92-L100】.  Their social behavior shows they are **gregarious**, meaning “fond of the company of others; sociable”【961129226621014†L30-L37】.  The passage concludes that humans are not the only species to value **camaraderie**, defined by Merriam‑Webster as “a feeling of friendliness, goodwill and familiarity among the people in a group”【727654263820785†L90-L92】.  “Circadian” refers to biological rhythms; “quotidian” means everyday; “solitary” is the opposite of gregarious; “individuality” and “seclusion” contradict the idea of valuing group interaction.

| Slot | Option | Meaning and evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| **Blank (i)** | **circadian** | Relating to 24‑hour biological cycles; not specifically day‑active. |
|  | **diurnal** | Active in the daytime【473955585669182†L92-L100】; matches vervet monkeys. **Correct.** |
|  | **quotidian** | Occurring every day; does not denote daytime activity. |
| **Blank (ii)** | **egregious** | Shockingly bad; opposite of friendly behavior. |
|  | **solitary** | Preferring to be alone; contrary to the monkeys’ social nature. |
|  | **gregarious** | Sociable or living in groups【961129226621014†L30-L37】; correct description. **Correct.** |
| **Blank (iii)** | **individuality** | Emphasizes being different or unique; opposite of group goodwill. |
|  | **camaraderie** | A feeling of friendliness and goodwill among members of a group【727654263820785†L90-L92】; fits the context. **Correct.** |
|  | **seclusion** | Isolation; opposite of social camaraderie. |
