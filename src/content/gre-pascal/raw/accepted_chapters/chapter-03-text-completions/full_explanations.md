# GRE Text Completion: Detailed Explanations (Questions 1–25)

This document provides detailed explanations for the first 25 Text Completion questions from the *GRE Pascal Book*.  Each entry states the correct answer(s), explains why it fits the context, and gives the meaning of each option, along with reasons the incorrect choices do not work.  Dictionary definitions are cited where useful.

## Q1

**Correct answer:** *healing*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence notes that ginger is “used by cooks and by everyone from seamen to housewives to treat coughs and upset stomachs.”  This clearly implies a **medicinal** or **curative** property.  *Healing* is defined as “tending to heal or cure; **curative**”【64597026019870†L88-L91】, making it the only choice that directly matches the idea of a medicinal herb.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

| Option | Meaning | Why it doesn’t fit |
|---|---|---|
| *Edible* | Fit or safe to be eaten【320512473551335†L88-L89】 | Describes something you can eat, but says nothing about medicinal effects. |
| *Injurious* | Causing injury or harm【211214878764115†L61-L72】 | The sentence praises ginger; describing it as harmful contradicts the context. |
| *Toxic* | Poisonous or containing poison【77065699228332†L57-L67】 | Also implies harm, which conflicts with the herb’s soothing properties. |
| *Detrimental* | Causing damage or harm【136211766824389†L63-L69】 | Likewise negative; ginger is helpful here, not damaging. |

## Q2

**Correct answer:** *winnow*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence states that “agents…” must discern and separate manuscripts worth reading from the flood of submissions.  *To winnow* means “to separate the good from the bad; to sift”【696445688619479†L70-L80】.  An agent’s job is literally to winnow promising manuscripts out of the slush pile.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

| Option | Meaning | Why it doesn’t fit |
|---|---|---|
| *Supplant* | To replace one thing with another【130721396620972†L61-L67】 | Replacing manuscripts does not describe the agent’s job; they must sift, not swap. |
| *Alter* | To change without changing the essence【653082157161592†L94-L99】 | Agents do not modify manuscripts in this context; they select them. |
| *Overhaul* | To repair or renovate completely【559206940739197†L59-L66】 | Again implies major changes rather than selection. |
| *Revamp* | To change or arrange again so as to improve【506486173999591†L59-L63】 | Similar to overhaul; irrelevant to sorting manuscripts. |

## Q3

**Correct answer:** *(i) offset, (ii) established*.

**Why these answers fit:**  Conservationists supported creating wildlife refuges because such refuges **offset** the loss of natural wetlands and thereby help to preserve biodiversity.  *Offset* means to counterbalance or compensate for something【25861476712145†L99-L111】.  The second blank describes what the refuges did: they were not dismantled or surrendered, but **established** (i.e., created or founded).  To *establish* is “to start or create something that will continue”【990943710053309†L63-L69】.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

| Pair | Problems |
|---|---|
| *account for / surrendered* | *Account for* means to explain; it does not fit the idea of replacing losses.  *Surrendered* suggests giving up refuges rather than creating them. |
| *annihilate / dismantled* | *Annihilate* means to destroy completely【873149068770381†L59-L61】, the opposite of replacing losses.  *Dismantled* means taken apart, again the opposite of establishing refuges. |

## Q4

**Correct answer:** *(i) denounced, (ii) neglecting*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The scientific organization criticized the newspaper for covering “psychic predictions” instead of major research.  *Denounced* means to criticize or condemn publicly【334264110554759†L61-L65】; this matches the organization’s denunciation.  The second blank contrasts the faddish psychic coverage with “neglecting” serious research.  To *neglect* means to ignore or fail to care for.

**Why the other options are wrong:**  
*Celebrated / neglecting* – “Celebrated” means praised【917765539874742†L83-L85】; the organization clearly did not praise the paper.  
*Promoted / preferring* – These words imply positive support and a choice in favor of something rather than a criticism.  
*Opting* suggests choosing intentionally; the context is about failing to report research rather than choosing psychics deliberately.

## Q5

**Correct answer:** *jaded*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence says that technological progress is so rapid that consumers quickly become unimpressed by innovations.  *Jaded* describes someone “tired or bored after too much of something”【678637562065334†L40-L88】.  This fits the idea of consumers becoming blasé about once‐amazing devices.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

| Option | Meaning | Why it doesn’t fit |
|---|---|---|
| *Flabbergasted* | Extremely astonished | Consumers become bored, not astonished. |
| *Miffed* | Annoyed or offended | Doesn’t capture boredom; implies irritation. |
| *Wary* | Cautious or suspicious | Doesn’t match becoming unimpressed; suggests fear. |
| *Astounded* | Amazed | Again the opposite of jaded. |

## Q6

**Correct answer:** *censures*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence says the book “celebrates but also censures the author’s own role.”  *To censure* means to formally criticize.  This paradox—celebrating yet criticizing—makes the book surprising.

**Why the other options are wrong:**  
*Replicates* would mean copies; *condones* means approves; *documents* is neutral; *dismisses* means rejects completely.  None fit the idea of simultaneously praising and criticizing the author’s role.

## Q7

**Correct answer:** *(i) conciliatory, (ii) confrontational*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The sentence contrasts the new leader with her predecessor.  She is described as more **conciliatory**, meaning she seeks to reconcile or appease【582115645364866†L86-L90】, so her term will be less **confrontational** (less hostile).  The blanks therefore must be opposing qualities.

**Why the other options are wrong:**  
Other adjectives such as *phlegmatic*, *factious*, or *perfunctory* do not contrast with “confrontational” as clearly.  Likewise, filling the second blank with *divisive* or *deliberative* does not mirror the change implied.

## Q8

**Correct answer:** *(i) instantaneously, (ii) onset*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The sentence says that sleep arrives **instantaneously** (immediately) despite some people insisting on clues indicating its **onset** (beginning).  The contrast is between a sudden arrival and subtle warnings.

**Why the other options are wrong:**  
*Gradually / resurgence* would imply sleep comes slowly and returns after a pause—contradicting “arrives instantly.”  *Surreptitiously* (secretly) does not fit the context, and *course* or *disappearance* do not describe the beginning of sleep.  The correct pair balances the idea of a sudden arrival and a mere onset of clues.

## Q9

**Correct answer:** *mended*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The Hubble Space Telescope had a flawed mirror; the device described in the sentence corrected its vision.  *To mend* means to repair something that is broken, aligning with fixing the telescope’s flaw.  The other options—*lengthened*, *frayed*, *transformed*, *replaced*—do not accurately depict correction of a mirror flaw.  In particular, *transformed* implies complete change (too broad), and *replaced* would suggest swapping the telescope, not repairing it.

## Q10

**Correct answer:** *glacial*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The auditors’ expression is described as unmoved, hinting at extreme coldness.  *Glacial* means extremely cold, often metaphorically describing an icy demeanor.  The other options—*ardent* (passionate), *fevered* (excited), *arduous* (difficult), *arid* (dry)—do not capture the cold, unsmiling quality.

## Q11

**Correct answer:** *(i) razing, (ii) salvageable*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The speaker objects to the mass **razing** (demolition) of buildings that could be **salvageable**.  The sentence contrasts destruction with preservation.  Thus, the first blank must mean tearing down (razing), and the second must indicate something that can be saved (salvageable).  Other options like *constructing* or *pristine* do not reflect that contrast.

## Q12

**Correct answer:** *(i) maintain, (ii) vulnerable*.

**Why these answers fit:**  Communal nests help small reptiles **maintain** body heat, but they leave the animals more **vulnerable** to predators.  This describes a trade‑off: one benefit (heat) against increased risk.  Other combinations—such as *relax / invincible* or *shed / exposed*—do not express this relationship.

## Q13

**Correct answer:** *innocuous*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence says that despite claims of racial undertones, the movie seemed **innocuous** (harmless).  Thus, the word must mean harmless or inoffensive.  Options like *volatile* (likely to explode), *provocative* (intended to provoke), or *iconic* (widely admired) do not fit.  *Dubious* suggests doubt, not harmlessness.

## Q14

**Correct answer:** *an indomitable*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The biography portrays her as someone who overcomes great odds; *indomitable* means impossible to subdue or defeat.  It aligns with the theme of triumphing over adversity.  Other adjectives like *capricious* (whimsical), *adroit* (skillful), or *obstinate* (stubborn) do not capture heroic perseverance.

## Q15

**Correct answer:** *(i) preeminent, (ii) surreptitious*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The treaty is described as the **preeminent** (most important) document for species conservation, and it deters illegal, **surreptitious** (secretive) fishing.  Other options like *mundane* or *opulent* don’t fit the context, and **surreptitious** specifically refers to illicit, secret fishing, whereas alternatives like *ostentatious* (showy) or *palatable* (acceptable) are unrelated.

## Q16

**Correct answer:** *(i) substantiate, (ii) speculations*.

**Why these answers fit:**  New data on whales helped **substantiate** (support with evidence) earlier **speculations** (conjectures) about their diving habits.  Options like *dispel / observations* or *misinterpret / findings* would not reflect confirmation; the sentence describes confirming earlier guesses with data.

## Q17

**Correct answer:** *myopic*.

**Why this answer fits:**  Management pursued short‑term profit at the expense of long‑term company health.  *Myopic* literally means nearsighted and metaphorically denotes narrow‑mindedness or lack of foresight【855218129235699†L34-L40】.  Other options like *astute* (shrewd) or *prescient* (far-sighted) contradict the criticism.  *Belligerent* (hostile) does not fit either.

## Q18

**Correct answer:** *obsequious*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence describes the person’s slavish, fawning behavior toward the VIP.  *Obsequious* means excessively eager to please or obedient【151513051875164†L18-L27】.  Other options—*forthright*, *curmudgeonly*, *economical*, *aloof*—do not convey fawning submissiveness.

## Q19

**Correct answer:** *(i) exploitation, (ii) husbandry*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The passage criticizes the **exploitation** (overuse) of resources and urges replacing it with **husbandry**, meaning careful management or conservation.  This captures the shift from reckless use to stewardship.  Options like *preservation / obsession* or *cultivation / demise* don’t align with the intent.

## Q20

**Correct answer:** *(i) fertile, (ii) barren*.

**Why these answers fit:**  Desertification turns once **fertile** fields into **barren** wastelands.  The sentence contrasts productive soil with emptiness.  Options like *placid / arid* or *hospitable / charred* fail to capture the agricultural context.

## Q21

**Correct answer:** *apportioned*.

**Why this answer fits:**  In the United States, fandom is fairly evenly **apportioned** (distributed) among a few sports.  The context is about division of fan loyalty.  Words like *exaggerated*, *offered*, or *monopolized* do not convey even distribution.

## Q22

**Correct answer:** *delusion*.

**Why this answer fits:**  The sentence calls interstellar travel a collective fantasy.  *Delusion* means a false belief or illusion.  Other options such as *conversion*, *revelation*, or *hypothesis* do not express the idea of a mass illusion.

## Q23

**Correct answer:** *(i) crowned, (ii) dismissed*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The passage notes that Shakespeare has been **crowned** (hailed) as the greatest playwright yet sometimes **dismissed** (rejected) as undereducated.  The juxtaposition emphasises his paradoxical reputation.  Alternatives like *lauded / revered* or *elevated / respected* do not capture this dichotomy as well.

## Q24

**Correct answer:** *(i) complications, (ii) stouter, (iii) a concomitant*.

**Why these answers fit:**  Building very tall towers poses **complications**.  As towers grow taller they require **stouter** (stronger) bases; a **concomitant** (accompanying) issue is that larger tower sections cannot be transported on existing roads.  Other options either do not describe accompanying problems or mischaracterise the structural challenges.

## Q25

**Correct answer:** *(i) precarious, (ii) failing, (iii) declines*.

**Why these answers fit:**  The sentence says the warbler’s status is **precarious** (unstable), its numbers have been **failing** for years, and biologists are studying these **declines**.  The three blanks reinforce the theme of a species in trouble.  Alternatives such as *secure / flourishing / improvements* would contradict the threatened status.
