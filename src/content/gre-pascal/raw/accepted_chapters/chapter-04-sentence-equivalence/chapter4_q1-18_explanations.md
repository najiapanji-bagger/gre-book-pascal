# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence (Questions 1‑18)

## Question 1
* **Prompt:** The colonists faced a long, difficult winter and needed to **____** their existing resources.  The blank must mean **manage or conserve** what they already had.
* **Reasoning:** To **steward** something is to act as its manager; Merriam‑Webster notes that the verb means “to act as a steward for: **manage**”【224297962864367†L125-L134】.  To **husband** resources means to manage them prudently and economically; the dictionary defines this verb as “to **manage** prudently and economically” and “to use sparingly; **conserve**”【231885823576564†L116-L126】.  Both words capture the need to carefully manage supplies through the winter.
* **Answer:** **steward**, **husband**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Peddle* means to sell goods, not to conserve them. *Upend* means to overturn, and *procure* means to obtain more resources, whereas the sentence emphasizes using what already exists. *Self* and *procure* do not convey careful management or conservation.

## Question 2
* **Prompt:** At work she is far less **___** than she is around her friends, but her staff occasionally sees her in a volatile state.  The blank should describe a person who is **changeable or unpredictable** in mood or behavior.
* **Reasoning:** The adjective **capricious** describes someone governed by sudden, unpredictable impulses【719193257665601†L88-L100】.  **Fickle** likewise refers to a person whose opinions or feelings change suddenly and without good reason【57378905178504†L59-L63】.  Both words indicate volatility and inconsistency, matching the contrast between her restrained work demeanor and her mercurial behavior with friends.
* **Answer:** **capricious**, **fickle**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Pretentious* means showily self‑important; *informal* and *direct* describe manner, not mood swings. *Explicit* means clear and fully expressed.  None of these words capture the notion of unpredictability or volatility.

## Question 3
* **Prompt:** “In Inuit culture, elaborate carving has often been used to enhance **___** objects such as harpoon heads and other tools.”  The blank needs an adjective meaning **designed for use rather than decoration**.
* **Reasoning:** **Utilitarian** means “having regard to **utility or usefulness** rather than beauty or ornamentation”【382640963408012†L30-L35】.  **Functional** is defined as “designed or developed chiefly from the point of view of **use**”【852565490834573†L100-L108】.  Harpoon heads and tools are utilitarian/functional objects that serve a purpose; the sentence notes that they were artistically carved despite their functional nature.
* **Answer:** **utilitarian**, **functional**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Decorative* and *ornamental* describe something whose only purpose is beauty; that is the opposite of the utilitarian tools mentioned. *Domestic* and *manufactured* are too broad and do not specifically mean “intended for use.”

## Question 4
* **Prompt:** “The music of the late 1970s is often described as **___** despite the notable exception of a few innovators in the punk and hip‑hop scenes.”  Here the blank must mean **unoriginal or lacking novelty**.
* **Reasoning:** A **derivative** work is “taken or created from something original” and is thus **unoriginal**【767922101838434†L78-L82】.  **Trite** refers to ideas or remarks that are boring because they have been said too often【722429524590823†L213-L215】.  By contrasting the mainstream music with innovators, the sentence implies that much of the music was derivative/trite.
* **Answer:** **derivative**, **trite**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Inspired* and *visionary* suggest originality, the opposite of the criticism. *Enigmatic* and *cerebral* describe mysterious or intellectual qualities, not lack of originality.

## Question 5
* **Prompt:** A field trip was organized so that this troupe of **___** dancers could observe the real masters of their art.  The blank must describe dancers who are **inexperienced or new**.
* **Reasoning:** A **fledgling** is someone who is new and without experience【484454138718111†L111-L163】.  A **novice** is “a person who is not experienced in a job or situation”【58053753515396†L59-L63】.  Both words describe beginners, fitting the context of inexperienced dancers needing to learn from experts.
* **Answer:** **fledgling**, **novice**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Seasoned* and *expert* describe experienced dancers, which contradicts the notion of needing instruction. *Torpid* means sluggish, and *lithe* means flexible; neither corresponds to lack of experience.

## Question 6
* **Prompt:** One educator ignited controversy by calling the public schools a “rotten barrel” in need of **___** reform.  The blank must indicate reform that affects the **entire system**.
* **Reasoning:** A **systemic** problem is one that involves a **whole system**【318393011830478†L122-L124】.  **Total** as an adjective means “comprising or constituting a whole: **entire**”【880919465823449†L93-L103】.  Calling for systemic/total reform emphasizes that partial measures will not fix the “rotten barrel.”
* **Answer:** **systemic**, **total**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Little* and *partial* describe reforms that are limited in scope, contradicting the call for comprehensive change. *Pretentious* and *superficial* critique style rather than scope.

## Question 7
* **Prompt:** After a long, hard practice in the summer sun, the players were visibly **___**.  The blank must mean **tired or weakened**.
* **Reasoning:** To **flag** (as a verb) means to become tired, weaker or less effective【294716583469515†L381-L387】.  To **enervate** someone is to make them feel weak and without energy【129810610219631†L61-L62】.  Players who are flagging/enervated after strenuous practice are visibly exhausted.
* **Answer:** **flagging**, **enervated**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Hale* and *vigorous* mean healthy or strong—the opposite of fatigued. *Repugnant* and *loathsome* convey disgust, not tiredness.

## Question 8
* **Prompt:** Many people mistakenly believe humans are naturally **___** to distrust those outside their group; anthropologists have shown xenophobia is learned.  The blank should describe being **inclined or predisposed** to do something.
* **Reasoning:** **Inclined** means “likely or wanting to do something”【410086864338584†L61-L63】.  **Disposed** similarly describes someone willing or eager to do a particular thing【372747048358363†L211-L220】.  The mistaken belief is that people are inherently inclined/disposed to xenophobia; research shows otherwise.
* **Answer:** **inclined**, **disposed**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Indoctrinated*, *forced*, *taught* and *instructed* all imply external teaching, which contradicts the claim of natural predisposition.  The sentence explicitly contrasts learned behavior with an innate inclination.

## Question 9
* **Prompt:** Nothing evoked memories of her grandmother’s house like the **___** of scents associated with the variety of dishes at the holiday feast.  The blank must describe a **mixture** of aromas.
* **Reasoning:** A **medley** is “a mixture of things not usually placed together; heterogeneous collection”【602094311849946†L253-L255】.  A **mélange** is defined as “a mixture often of incongruous elements”【384003432149861†L78-L80】.  Together they capture the sense of diverse scents blending in the kitchen.
* **Answer:** **medley**, **mélange**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Paucity*, *dearth* and *rarity* signify scarcity, not abundance. *Anomaly* refers to something unusual, not a mixture.

## Question 10
* **Prompt:** Unlike the politician’s earlier evasions and equivocations, this latest statement is **___** lie.  The blank must mean **plain and undisguised**.
* **Reasoning:** A **bald** statement is in plain language with no unnecessary detail or elaboration【900011369056316†L116-L119】.  **Overt** actions are “done or shown publicly or in an obvious way and not secret”【330743610554779†L59-L63】.  Thus a bald/overt lie is blatant and unhidden.
* **Answer:** **a bald**, **an overt**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Tacit* and *implicit* indicate something unspoken; *fleeting* means short‑lived; *abstract* refers to conceptual ideas.  None of these describe a lie that is openly expressed.

## Question 11
* **Prompt:** Where gay and lesbian individuals were once ignored by mainstream media and marketing companies, they are now being **___** as the new frontier in consumer spending.  The blank should mean **publicly praised or advertised**.
* **Reasoning:** To **tout** something is to “advertise, talk about, or praise something or someone repeatedly … to encourage people to like, accept, or buy”【892817784720393†L61-L69】.  To **promote** in the marketing sense means to encourage people to like, buy, or support something【812458668383092†L61-L67】.  Both words capture marketing efforts that praise a target group to entice advertisers.
* **Answer:** **touted**, **promoted**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Subverted* means undermined, and *predicted* relates to forecasting. *Examined* and *scrutinized* suggest analysis, not advertising; neither describes positive marketing attention.

## Question 12
* **Prompt:** For most of the 20th century, American political debates were pragmatic rather than ideological; candidates argued about whether programs were **___**.  The blank must mean **practicable or capable of working**.
* **Reasoning:** **Feasible** means something is able to be made, done or achieved【735528347115879†L61-L63】.  **Viable** refers to something that is able to work as intended or to succeed【582154599197671†L61-L67】.  Debating whether a program is feasible/viable fits the pragmatic focus on practical possibilities.
* **Answer:** **feasible**, **viable**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Partisan* and *prejudiced* relate to ideological bias; *voluble* means talkative; *innocuous* means harmless.  None reflect the idea of workability or practicality.

## Question 13
* **Prompt:** With almost 40 titles to her name, the popular novelist has **___** imagination and is never at a loss for new ideas, though the quality of her work is uneven.  The blank should mean **highly productive or fertile**.
* **Reasoning:** **Prolific** describes someone marked by abundant inventiveness or productivity【144319131587914†L85-L87】.  **Fecund** likewise means fruitful or intellectually productive【729064045682780†L91-L102】.  Both convey a writer who generates many ideas and works.
* **Answer:** **a prolific**, **a fecund**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Verbose* means wordy; *exemplary* means outstanding; *ingenious* suggests cleverness; *profligate* means wasteful.  None capture sustained high output.

## Question 14
* **Prompt:** Representative government arose partly because too many monarchs made **___** decisions without regard for precedent.  The blank must convey **unpredictable or arbitrary** actions.
* **Reasoning:** **Capricious** implies decisions governed by sudden, irrational impulses【719193257665601†L88-L100】.  **Erratic** means not following a regular pattern and happening at unexpected times【655190698579609†L213-L217】.  Monarchs who made capricious/erratic choices provoked the demand for more predictable rule.
* **Answer:** **capricious**, **erratic**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Considered* suggests careful thought, *pessimistic* reflects outlook, *malicious* and *insidious* imply harm.  None denote unpredictability.

## Question 15
* **Prompt:** Because the Lewis and Clark expedition was conceived primarily as a mapping project, officials were **___** by the wealth of information on myriad topics that the explorers gathered.  The blank should express **astonishment or amazement**.
* **Reasoning:** **Flabbergasted** is defined as feeling or showing intense shock, surprise, or wonder—utterly astonished【310812523570065†L80-L82】.  To **bedazzle** someone is to impress them very much【825193255238828†L59-L64】.  The huge trove of unexpected information left officials flabbergasted and bedazzled.
* **Answer:** **flabbergasted**, **bedazzled**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Aggravated* means annoyed; *crushed* implies defeat; *bored* and *disappointed* indicate negative reactions.  None convey positive astonishment.

## Question 16
* **Prompt:** Although accommodating in person, George Orwell **___** defended his political positions in print.  The blank must indicate **persistent, determined** defense.
* **Reasoning:** **Tenacious** describes holding tightly to something or keeping an opinion in a determined way【279058835667170†L59-L63】.  **Dogged** means very determined to continue doing something even when it is difficult【736285811907007†L59-L63】.  When used adverbially (tenaciously/doggedly), they convey stubborn persistence in defending one’s views.
* **Answer:** **tenaciously**, **doggedly**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Obsequiously* and *sycophantically* describe fawning behavior, not stubborn advocacy.  *Inadvertently* means unintentionally; *idiosyncratically* means peculiarly.  These do not fit Orwell’s fierce defense.

## Question 17
* **Prompt:** Given the influx of information via social media, the only way a person can function effectively is to **___** to metaphorically separate the wheat from the chaff.  The blank must denote **removing unwanted information**.
* **Reasoning:** To **winnow** means to get rid of something undesirable or unwanted; to remove【312156047577236†L92-L103】.  To **eliminate** is “to remove or take away someone or something”【147307385410868†L61-L67】.  Both verbs describe the process of filtering out unnecessary information.
* **Answer:** **winnow**, **eliminate**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Delete* removes entire items rather than sorting; *prioritize* orders items rather than discarding them. *Respond* and *retaliate* involve reaction, not filtering.

## Question 18
* **Prompt:** Although historically painters used toxic elements like lead and cadmium in their paints, **___** number of artists lived into their seventies, eighties and even nineties.  The blank must indicate a result that is **surprisingly large or unexpected**.
* **Reasoning:** To **flabbergast** is to shock or surprise someone very much【513873640003476†L46-L52】; used adjectivally, **flabbergasting** describes something that causes such astonishment.  To **confound** is to confuse and very much surprise someone【604715409773918†L63-L64】.  It is therefore **flabbergasting** or **confounding** that so many painters lived long lives despite exposure to toxic pigments.
* **Answer:** **a flabbergasting**, **a confounding**.
* **Why others are wrong:** *Incomprehensible* suggests impossible to understand; *impossible* and *dismaying* imply negativity, while *enlightening* connotes positive insight.  Only flabbergasting and confounding convey astonished surprise at the long lifespans.
