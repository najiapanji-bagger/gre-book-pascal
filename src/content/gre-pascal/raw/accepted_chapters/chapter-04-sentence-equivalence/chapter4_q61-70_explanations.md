# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence (Questions 61‑70)

The questions in this section ask you to select two words that both fit logically and produce sentences with essentially the same meaning.  Below each question the correct pair of choices is explained, definitions of the key words are provided, and the other options are briefly addressed.

## Question 61

**Prompt:**  The bridge was retrofitted with stabilizing devices, some of which ___ its aesthetics.

**Correct choices:** **impair, compromise**  
These verbs both mean “to damage or lessen.”  Collins notes that if something **impairs** something such as an ability or a way something works, “it **damages it or makes it worse**”【150463926240931†L214-L218】.  Merriam‑Webster likewise defines **compromise** (verb) in one sense as “to **cause the impairment** of” something【486745210490923†L154-L163】.  The sentence explains that the new devices harm the bridge’s appearance, so either verb works and produces the same meaning.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Resist* means to oppose or withstand; it would not describe harming the bridge’s appearance.
- *Improve* and *enhance* would indicate making the aesthetics better, the opposite of the intended meaning.
- *Restore* suggests returning to an original state; this does not fit the context of the new devices.

## Question 62

**Prompt:**  Historically, cosmic objects have struck Earth, yet two centuries ago the idea that objects orbiting the Sun could collide with Earth was widely ___.

**Correct choices:** **doubted, disbelieved**  
To **doubt** something is “to not feel certain or confident about something” or “to think that something is not probable”【216331717078316†L192-L197】.  To **disbelieve** means “to **reject as false**; refuse to accept”【38515709113182†L18-L36】.  Both choices convey that many people did **not accept** the possibility of collisions, which is the point of the sentence.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Valued* and *aggrandized* imply appreciation or praise, which conflicts with the idea of widespread skepticism.
- *Disseminated* and *promulgated* mean “spread widely,” but the sentence says the idea was not accepted.

## Question 63

**Prompt:**  A quotation misrepresented the mayor’s position; the misquotation was ___.

**Correct choices:** **spurious, bogus**  
Merriam‑Webster defines **spurious** as something “outwardly similar…without having its **genuine qualities**; **false**”【251520203963165†L91-L100】.  The Cambridge Dictionary defines **bogus** as something “**false** … or not real”【305456570750436†L59-L64】.  Both words describe a **fake** or **fraudulent** quotation; they reinforce that the misquote did not genuinely reflect the mayor’s view.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Inflammatory* means provoking anger; it does not necessarily mean false.
- *Abbreviated* means shortened, not false.
- *Abominable* and *egregious* describe something morally bad or shocking; they don’t indicate falsity.

## Question 64

**Prompt:**  Torpey’s study turns the seemingly ___ topic of the passport into a fascinating subject.

**Correct choices:** **banal, insipid**  
Dictionary.com defines **banal** as “**devoid of freshness or originality; hackneyed; trite**”【194838950136292†L31-L33】.  The Collins dictionary explains that when someone or something is **insipid**, it is “not exciting or interesting; dull”【111639208892028†L224-L232】.  Both words describe a **dull or uninteresting** topic, which is why the study’s ability to make it fascinating is noteworthy.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Ironic* and *witty* involve humor; they don’t signal dullness.
- *Provocative* and *stimulating* imply something exciting or thought‑provoking, the opposite of the idea.

## Question 65

**Prompt:**  Britain is attractive to advertisers because it is a ___ market; there is no need to tailor advertisements for different regions.

**Correct choices:** **homogeneous, uniform**  
Merriam‑Webster defines **homogeneous** as “of the **same or a similar kind or nature**” and “of **uniform structure or composition throughout**”【923115448897158†L86-L100】.  **Uniform** means “having always the **same form, manner, or degree: not varying**”【684139269766800†L96-L100】.  Together these words emphasize that Britain is **consistent across regions**, so a single advertising campaign suffices.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Global*, *vast* and *immense* describe size, not consistency.
- *Uncomplicated* relates to complexity but doesn’t convey sameness.

## Question 66

**Prompt:**  The band’s strategy of leisurely explorations over a funk beat is surprisingly ___; a concert from 1972 sounds as if it could have been performed today.

**Correct choices:** **foresighted, prescient**  
WordWeb defines **foresighted** as “planning prudently for the **future**”【646778897921307†L28-L33】.  The Cambridge Dictionary defines **prescient** as “**knowing or suggesting correctly what will happen in the future**”【165359633547358†L61-L64】.  These words show that the band’s style anticipated future tastes, making their music seem contemporary decades later.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Fortuitous* and *serendipitous* mean accidental and do not imply foresight.
- *Popular* and *lucrative* relate to success or profitability, not anticipation.

## Question 67

**Prompt:**  Factory production made the absence of imperfections commonplace, so the ___ of handmade goods were now cherished.

**Correct choices:** **blemishes, defects**  
Merriam‑Webster defines a **blemish** as “a noticeable **imperfection**, especially one that seriously **impairs appearance**”【902156364595309†L93-L103】.  It defines a **defect** as “an **imperfection** or abnormality that impairs quality, function, or utility”【180741712652499†L93-L100】.  In the context of mass‑produced perfection, the slight flaws of handmade items become appealing markers of craftsmanship.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Advantages*, *benefits*, and *revivals* are positive attributes, not flaws.
- *Pretenses* refers to false displays, not imperfections.

## Question 68

**Prompt:**  The American Medical Association dominated the medical field and also the ___ professions of nursing and occupational therapy.

**Correct choices:** **kindred, affiliated**  
The Cambridge Dictionary defines **kindred** (adjective) as “**similar or related**”【213537220573330†L61-L64】.  Vocabulary.com explains that **affiliated** means things that are “**closely associated or connected** with one another”【467031351777280†L18-L21】.  Both words describe professions **related to medicine**, which the AMA also controlled.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Commensurate* and *proportionate* refer to relative sizes, not relationships.
- *Imperative* and *voluntary* address necessity or choice, not relatedness.

## Question 69

**Prompt:**  The likenesses of languages around the Mediterranean were sufficient to ___ ease of movement both of men and of ideas; a Spanish song could easily be understood in Italy, and an Italian trader could feel at home in France.

**Correct choices:** **facilitate, enable**  
To **facilitate** something is “to **make something possible or easier**”【783898075464897†L63-L68】.  To **enable** means “to **make someone able to do something, or to make something possible**”【677460050969689†L61-L64】.  Because the similar languages made travel and communication **easier**, these two verbs fit naturally and produce equivalent sentences.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Clarify* means to make clear; it doesn’t convey easing movement.
- *Eliminate* and *aggravate* are opposite in meaning (to remove or to worsen), which doesn’t fit.
- *Hinder* would impede movement, the opposite of what the sentence implies.

## Question 70

**Prompt:**  Mr. Hirsch says he will preserve the foundation’s support of ___ thinkers—individuals who are going against the trends in a field or an acknowledged set of opinions.

**Correct choices:** **dissenting, heterodox**  
Merriam‑Webster notes that to **dissent** is “to **differ in opinion**”【130682640757592†L94-L105】, so a **dissenting** thinker disagrees with the prevailing view.  Dictionary.com defines **heterodox** as “**not in accordance with established or accepted doctrines or opinions; unorthodox**”【52243682022234†L29-L33】.  Both words describe thinkers who challenge orthodox views, which matches the description.

**Why the other options are wrong:**

- *Integrative* and *doctrinaire* suggest adhering to a system or doctrine; they don’t connote going against the trend.
- *Dogmatic* and *unbending* refer to rigid adherence to beliefs, not challenging them.
