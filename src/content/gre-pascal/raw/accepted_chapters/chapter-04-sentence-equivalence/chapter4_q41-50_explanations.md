# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence (Questions 41‑50)

## Question 41
* **Prompt:**  Scientists debated which bird species first succumbed to a mutated virus.  What *is* known is that the mutation made the pathogen lethal to a very large number of related species, so the blank must describe a great quantity.
* **Reasoning:**  A **myriad** is “a very large number of something”【938387177630390†L61-L65】.  A **plethora** likewise means “an ample amount or number” or an abundance【701466968039187†L86-L90】.  Either word shows that the slightly altered virus killed many related species, conveying the idea of huge numbers.
* **Answer:** **a myriad**, **a plethora**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *A modicum* means a small amount, while *a dearth* or *a lack* suggest scarcity.  *A surplus* connotes excess but not necessarily widespread death.  None of these match the emphasis on “very many.”

## Question 42
* **Prompt:**  An opportunist is described as constantly seeking shady deals and cons.  The blank should label her lack of moral principles.
* **Reasoning:**  **Unprincipled** is defined as “lacking or not based on moral scruples or principles”【562945726903176†L31-L33】 and as “lacking moral principles; unscrupulous”【562945726903176†L47-L49】.  **Unscrupulous** similarly means “not scrupulous; unprincipled”【487317449753160†L84-L86】.  Both words characterize someone willing to act dishonestly to serve herself, aptly describing a shady opportunist.
* **Answer:** **unprincipled**, **unscrupulous**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Unqualified* means lacking necessary credentials, not lacking morality.  *Alluring* and *attractive* refer to appeal, and *unprecedented* means without precedent; none imply unethical behavior.

## Question 43
* **Prompt:**  Reviews quickly revealed that a book’s argument had holes.  The blank must describe the argument as lacking or defective.
* **Reasoning:**  **Wanting** means “lacking or absent” and “deficient in some part”【292596296264082†L29-L35】.  **Flawed** means “having a defect or imperfection”【493676019947181†L77-L80】.  Both words capture an argument that is missing key elements and is thus defective.
* **Answer:** **wanting**, **flawed**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Convoluted* and *complex* imply difficulty but not inadequacy.  *Unintelligible* and *penetrating* concern clarity or insight rather than completeness.

## Question 44
* **Prompt:**  The sentence notes that kidney stones produce a sensation compared to the agony of childbirth.  The blank must convey extreme pain.
* **Reasoning:**  **Excruciating** is defined as “causing great pain or anguish; agonizing”【358904557672550†L83-L85】.  **Torturous** means “pertaining to, involving, or causing torture or suffering”【352284465865139†L31-L33】.  Both words emphasize severe, agonizing pain, fitting a description of kidney stone discomfort.
* **Answer:** **excruciating**, **torturous**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Soothing* and *inoffensive* are the opposite of painful.  *Painstaking* describes thoroughness, while *tortuous* (with two “u”s) refers to something winding or convoluted rather than painful.

## Question 45
* **Prompt:**  To hide their misdeeds, celebrities use methods “as ___ as those of a spy ring.”  The blank should mean concealed from view or hidden.
* **Reasoning:**  To **shroud** something is to cut it off from view or “veil” it【935764441997748†L144-L155】.  To **cloak** something, as a verb, is “to cover or hide with or as if with a cloak”【969783357670431†L116-L118】.  Both verbs suggest disguising or concealing actions—just like spies operating in secrecy.
* **Answer:** **shrouded**, **cloaked**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Draconian* and *arduous* indicate harshness or difficulty, not secrecy.  *Conspicuous* is the opposite of hidden.  *Fanciful* implies imagination rather than concealment.

## Question 46
* **Prompt:**  Carbon‑fiber frames represented a break from decades‑old bicycle technology.  The blank must convey genuine newness or a major change.
* **Reasoning:**  **Innovation** is defined as “a new idea, method, or device” and also “the introduction of something new”【180519652739464†L82-L97】.  **Transformation** refers to “a change or alteration, especially a radical one”【710182133102764†L30-L72】.  Both words emphasize significant novelty and change, making them suitable for describing carbon‑fiber frames as a genuine advance.
* **Answer:** **innovation**, **transformation**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Antiquity* suggests oldness, *flexibility* concerns pliability, and *reverence* and *seriousness* relate to respect or tone; none convey the idea of novel change.

## Question 47
* **Prompt:**  Although an “unlikely hero” was eventually vindicated, his findings were initially greeted with negativity.  The blank should denote scorn and disbelief.
* **Reasoning:**  **Derision** means ridicule or mockery【956424585548813†L30-L36】.  **Incredulity** is “the quality or state of being incredulous,” i.e., disbelief【235172526886676†L86-L87】.  Reviews that treat a person’s work with derision and incredulity dismiss it as laughable or unbelievable, matching the sense of initial negative reception.
* **Answer:** **derision**, **incredulity**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Accolades*, *commendations*, *sympathy* and *jubilation* all signal approval or celebration, not scorn.  *Sympathy* expresses pity, which does not fit reviewers’ reaction to flawed findings.

## Question 48
* **Prompt:**  Despite being delayed and over budget, the software delivered all the capabilities the specification had claimed; however, the delay meant competitors had caught up.  The blank should describe what the specification did regarding those capabilities.
* **Reasoning:**  To **boast** (transitive verb) can mean “to possess and often call attention to” something【338683979267553†L140-L146】, and, more generally, to speak of something with excessive pride【338683979267553†L117-L124】.  A specification that “boasted of” features asserted them proudly.  To **promise** something is “to pledge to do, bring about, or provide”【888495335485525†L123-L130】 or, as a noun, a declaration that assures performance【888495335485525†L94-L99】.  If the specification boasted of or promised certain capabilities, the completed software delivering them would meet those claims.
* **Answer:** **boasted of**, **promised**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Possessed* or *owned* simply state that the specification had something, not that it claimed it.  *Updated* and *enhanced* imply improvement; the passage emphasizes that the finished software merely matched what was claimed, not that the specification changed.

## Question 49
* **Prompt:**  A mayor’s speech turned a bipartisan issue into a long fight between former allies.  The blank should describe speech that divides or alienates people.
* **Reasoning:**  To **alienate** someone is “to make [them] indifferent or hostile” or to turn them away【180763626190972†L31-L38】.  A **divisive** statement is one that is “causing or tending to cause disagreement or dissension”【881036094387647†L42-L44】.  An alienating or divisive speech would transform a shared issue into a polarizing battle.
* **Answer:** **alienating**, **divisive**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Honest* and *refreshing* suggest candor and novelty.  *Lethargic* means sluggish, and *conventional* means ordinary.  None of these would turn a bipartisan issue into a heated fight.

## Question 50
* **Prompt:**  If cursive disappears from education, future generations will be unable to read historic documents.  The blank must describe such documents as hard or impossible to read.
* **Reasoning:**  **Unintelligible** means “not intelligible; not capable of being understood”【293209788026719†L30-L33】.  **Indecipherable** likewise means “not decipherable; illegible” and “not understandable”【982916668288747†L30-L33】.  If cursive handwriting becomes rare, letters, diaries and minutes would be unintelligible and indecipherable to those who cannot read it.
* **Answer:** **unintelligible**, **indecipherable**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Intellectual* and *humdrum* relate to intellectual matters or boredom, not readability.  *Meaningless* suggests the texts lack meaning rather than being hard to read, while *quotidian* implies everyday or commonplace.  Only unintelligible and indecipherable capture the idea of being unreadable.
