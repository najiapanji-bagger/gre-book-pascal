# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence (Questions 19‑30)

## Question 19
* **Prompt:**  The sentence describes a moderate activist who “still advocated …,” but to reach middle‑of‑the‑road voters she had to **___** her views.  The blank therefore needs a verb meaning “to moderate or hold back.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Restrain** means to stop someone from doing something or to keep an emotion under control【406227262619127†L216-L229】.  **Temper** as a verb means to moderate or mitigate and to soften or tone down【9864763288527†L78-L86】.  These words both describe limiting the expression of her opinions.  
* **Answer:** **restrain**, **temper**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Abridge* means “to shorten,” but one shortens a document, not one’s views.  *Amalgamate* means “to combine,” and *undermine* means “to damage,” both unsuitable here.  *Galvanize* means “to stimulate into action,” the opposite of moderation.

## Question 20
* **Prompt:**  The muted colors of an artwork suggest sobriety, yet “the overall effect is still undeniably ___.”  The blank must convey that despite somber colors the overall tone is lively.
* **Reasoning:**  **Vivacious** means lively, animated or spirited【850429160020467†L27-L31】.  **Mirthful** means full of laughter, humor or happiness【224437253847729†L61-L63】【213198051646287†L79-L83】.  Both words convey liveliness that contrasts with the muted colors.  
* **Answer:** **vivacious**, **mirthful**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Poignant* suggests sadness or touching emotion and would not contradict “muted colors.”  *Dull* and *lackluster* mean boring, and *benign* means gentle or harmless; none capture lively vibrancy.

## Question 21
* **Prompt:**  A “Mycenaean waist” refers to tiny waists in ancient drawings and does not **___** any characteristic of most modern, overweight people.  The blank should mean “to reflect or accurately show.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Represent** means to show or describe something or someone【941517744452587†L149-L156】 or to be a sign or symbol of something【941517744452587†L159-L163】.  **Mirror** as a verb means to represent something honestly or to be very similar to something【397890903749588†L134-L146】.  These words fit because the ancient ideal does not represent or mirror modern physiques.  
* **Answer:** **represent**, **mirror**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Deify* and *denigrate* involve praise or insult and are unrelated.  *Defame* means to slander and *distort* means to twist the truth; neither fits the idea of simply not reflecting current characteristics.

## Question 22
* **Prompt:**  A commentator’s analysis of a Supreme Court decision “was anything but ___; he parroted his ideological compatriots.”  “Anything but” indicates the blank must contrast with parroting others, so it should mean “original.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Novel** as an adjective means new and not resembling something formerly known or used【349584088081666†L134-L158】.  **Fresh** similarly means new or different【264967499235720†L61-L81】.  The commentator was not novel or fresh; he merely repeated others’ views.  
* **Answer:** **novel**, **fresh**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Derivative* and *hackneyed* (unoriginal) describe what the analysis actually was, not what it “was anything but.”  *Tendentious* means biased and *evenhanded* means fair; neither reflects the required “newness.”

## Question 23
* **Prompt:**  “Notwithstanding their ___ regarding other issues, township residents have consistently passed the board of education’s annual budget.”  The blank must refer to differences among residents that do not prevent them from passing the budget.
* **Reasoning:**  **Disagreement** is defined as objection or arguing about what should be done【684593140322846†L213-L230】.  **Divergence** means a situation in which two things become different or the difference between them increases, such as a “divergence of opinion”【131394836230124†L117-L123】.  Both express differences of opinion that contrast with the unanimity on the budget.  
* **Answer:** **disagreement**, **divergence**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Accord* and *consensus* mean agreement, the opposite of the intended contrast.  *Indecision* means inability to decide, and *enthusiasm* is eagerness; neither suggests conflicting viewpoints.

## Question 24
* **Prompt:**  Arthur Conan Doyle’s hero Sherlock Holmes uses deception just like his nemesis, proving that ___ is not necessarily evil.  The blank needs a noun meaning “skillful trickiness.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Cunning** is defined as crafty or dexterous skill in attaining an end【810010604998169†L92-L109】 and as the ability to mislead or trap (craft or slyness)【810010604998169†L145-L158】.  **Slyness** refers to a quality of being sneaky or craftiness; it is shrewdness demonstrated by being skilled in deception【535656162299002†L18-L34】.  Together they imply clever deception that may be morally neutral.  
* **Answer:** **cunning**, **slyness**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Immorality* and *wrongdoing* imply evil, not skill.  *Brilliance* and *judgment* are unrelated to trickery.

## Question 25
* **Prompt:**  The birth of septuplets led to articles presenting ___ accounts of the medical problems associated with multiple births.  The blank should indicate reports that are serious and sobering, not celebratory.
* **Reasoning:**  **Sobering** describes something that makes you feel serious or think about serious matters【525567184155791†L59-L65】.  **Subdued** means a color or light is not bright or, when applied to a person, quiet and less happy than usual【241300122834180†L61-L107】.  These descriptions fit “accounts of medical problems” that temper exuberance.  
* **Answer:** **sobering**, **subdued**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Empathetic* suggests compassion rather than seriousness; *ecstatic*, *maudlin* and *elated* indicate strong happiness or sentimentality, the opposite of sober coverage.

## Question 26
* **Prompt:**  The judge’s standing was shaken by false allegations but ultimately emerged ___, meaning it was left intact.
* **Reasoning:**  **Undiminished** means not reduced or lessened【673949171637977†L27-L33】.  **Undamaged** means not harmed or spoiled【255537381714493†L59-L65】.  These words convey that the judge’s reputation retained its full strength despite the allegations.  
* **Answer:** **undiminished**, **undamaged**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Unqualified* means absolute or not limited; *undecided* and *unresolved* refer to decisions, not reputations.  *Unprincipled* means lacking ethics, the opposite of what the sentence states.

## Question 27
* **Prompt:**  Modern agriculture has improved yields, yet despite heavy pesticide use, ___ losses to disease and pests occur every year.  The blank should mean “large” or “important.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Significant** means important or noticeable【866894390636579†L61-L70】, while **considerable** means large or of noticeable importance【499949240196938†L61-L65】.  Both emphasize substantial losses in spite of technological advances.  
* **Answer:** **significant**, **considerable**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Incongruous* means inconsistent; *reasonable* means fair; *equitable* means just; *fortuitous* means accidental.  None convey “large” or “important.”

## Question 28
* **Prompt:**  As a demanding reader, the novelist Chase was often the object of ___ analyses by his contemporaries.  The blank should describe criticism that is sharp and biting.
* **Reasoning:**  **Acerbic** describes something spoken or written in a direct, witty and cruel way【34185568429930†L61-L68】.  **Scathing** means being very critical or harsh【917551301702707†L213-L219】.  These words capture the tone of sharp literary analysis directed at the novelist.  
* **Answer:** **acerbic**, **scathing**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Exacting* means demanding; *copious* means abundant; *respectful* and *meticulous* imply care rather than harshness.

## Question 29
* **Prompt:**  “Her ___ should not be confused with miserliness: she has always been willing to assist those in need.”  The blank describes prudent, non‑wasteful spending.
* **Reasoning:**  **Frugality** is the quality of being prudent in saving and lacking wastefulness【409947035453024†L31-L33】.  **Thrift** is careful management of money【979824098650074†L94-L98】, with synonyms including frugality【979824098650074†L148-L158】.  These words indicate economical habits without suggesting selfishness.  
* **Answer:** **frugality**, **thrift**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Stinginess* suggests an unwillingness to spend even when appropriate (i.e., miserliness).  *Diffidence* means shyness; *narrow‑mindedness* and *intolerance* refer to prejudice, not fiscal prudence.

## Question 30
* **Prompt:**  Novice writers mistakenly think convoluted sentence structure mirrors thought—that the more convoluted the structure, the more ___ the ideas.  The blank should mean “complex and elaborate.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Intricate** means having many small parts arranged in a complicated way or having many complicated details that make something difficult to understand【865216663653744†L59-L70】.  **Byzantine**, when used in lowercase, means complex or intricate and characterized by elaborate scheming【381902867862264†L45-L50】.  These words together capture the notion that complicated sentences reflect complex thought.  
* **Answer:** **intricate**, **byzantine**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Engaged* and *essential* describe involvement or importance, not complexity.  *Fanciful* means imaginative; *inconsequential* means trivial.
