# Sentence Equivalence – Chapter 4 Questions 51–60

For each GRE sentence‐equivalence question below, two answer choices must be selected.  Both choices must produce sentences with essentially the same meaning and ensure the completed sentence fits the logical context.  Each explanation cites authoritative dictionary definitions to justify the correct synonyms.

## Question 51
**Prompt:** *Demagogues do not deserve full blame for last summer’s public hysteria: although they turned the mood to their political advantage, they did not actually \_\_\_\_ it.*  
**Choices:** oppose, subdue, **fuel**, **foment**, postpone, confirm  
**Correct pair:** **fuel** and **foment**  
**Reasoning:** The sentence says demagogues exploited public hysteria but did not *create or intensify* it.  The blank therefore must mean “to stir up or make worse.”  *Fuel* as a verb means **to make a situation become worse or more intense**【190244169691269†L225-L229】, while *foment* means **to instigate or foster (discord, rebellion, etc.)**【857823503563086†L31-L34】.  Both verbs describe deliberately stirring up trouble.  Other options are inappropriate: *oppose* or *subdue* would mean the demagogues resisted or calmed the hysteria; *postpone* or *confirm* do not fit the context.

## Question 52
**Prompt:** *Although relying on much of the recent scholarship on mammalian evolution, Lott’s book is a distinctly \_\_\_\_ and even idiosyncratic contribution to the field.*  
**Choices:** derivative, insipid, bland, **pioneering**, **innovative**, pedestrian  
**Correct pair:** **pioneering** and **innovative**  
**Reasoning:** Despite drawing on prior scholarship, the book is described as idiosyncratic and fresh.  *Pioneering* means **being among the first in any field of inquiry or activity; spearheading an initiative**【469486309727567†L27-L34】, while *innovative* means **introducing something new or different and using new methods or ideas**【275583544250070†L32-L44】.  Both words indicate originality.  The other choices (derivative, bland, pedestrian, insipid) suggest unoriginality or dullness and contradict the idea of an idiosyncratic contribution.

## Question 53
**Prompt:** *When Steven got angry about politics, whether arguing with his family or a coworker, it proved impossible to \_\_\_\_ him.*  
**Choices:** condemn, **pacify**, judge, incense, **mollify**, influence  
**Correct pair:** **pacify** and **mollify**  
**Reasoning:** The sentence describes calming someone who is angry.  *Pacify* means **to allay the anger or agitation of; to soothe or restore to a tranquil state**【44961579812467†L91-L109】.  Similarly, *mollify* means **to soothe in temper or disposition** or **to reduce intensity**【348945786903085†L91-L108】.  Both fill the blank with the idea of calming Steven down.  Condemn or judge would criticize him; incense would inflame his anger; influence is too vague.

## Question 54
**Prompt:** *Joyce’s *Finnegans Wake*, written in a style full of convoluted puns and obscure allusions, has a deserved reputation for linguistic \_\_\_\_.*  
**Choices:** elaborateness, **opacity**, meaninglessness, informality, uniqueness, **density**  
**Correct pair:** **opacity** and **density**  
**Reasoning:** The reputation arises because Joyce’s language is difficult to penetrate.  *Opacity* means that something is **difficult to understand**【588784477179885†L222-L229】.  *Dense* text can be **packed with complicated words and thoughts, making it difficult to get through**【713533821288435†L24-L28】.  Both words convey linguistic impenetrability.  *Elaborateness* suggests ornate detail but not necessarily unreadability; *meaninglessness* and *informality* mischaracterize Joyce’s deliberate complexity; *uniqueness* does not imply difficulty.

## Question 55
**Prompt:** *The financial situation in many European nations is \_\_\_\_ enough that even a small incident could lead to catastrophe.*  
**Choices:** calamitous, **unstable**, illegitimate, unsafe, **precarious**, cataclysmic  
**Correct pair:** **unstable** and **precarious**  
**Reasoning:** The sentence warns that a minor event could tip these finances into disaster, suggesting they are shaky or insecure.  *Unstable* is defined as **not firmly fixed; unsteady; lacking stability**【206765373215366†L32-L33】.  *Precarious* describes something **dependent on circumstances beyond one’s control; uncertain and liable to failure or catastrophe**【255663279380616†L31-L33】【255663279380616†L80-L82】.  Both words capture the fragility described.  *Calamitous* and *cataclysmic* mean disastrous, implying that catastrophe has already occurred; *illegitimate* and *unsafe* are irrelevant here.

## Question 56
**Prompt:** *While the argument for global warming may not be \_\_\_\_ by the record low temperatures reported this year, these data do not undermine the overall trend of steadily higher global temperatures.*  
**Choices:** **bolstered**, **fortified**, subverted, defined, stipulated, subordinated  
**Correct pair:** **bolstered** and **fortified**  
**Reasoning:** The clause indicates that unusually cold data do not *strengthen* the case for warming, but neither do they overturn it.  *Bolster* (verb) means **to increase or strengthen something such as someone’s confidence or position**【772548184132651†L214-L229】, and *fortify* means **to make strong; to strengthen and secure**【314458915466359†L86-L104】.  Both words convey strengthening the argument.  *Subverted* would imply undermining it; *defined*, *stipulated* or *subordinated* do not fit the sense of evidentiary support.

## Question 57
**Prompt:** *Last year it was discovered that the highly acclaimed *Friends* sitcom \_\_\_\_ part of several of its storylines from some similar *College Humor* sketches.*  
**Choices:** amalgamated, **filched**, indulged, combined, **poached**, assumed  
**Correct pair:** **filched** and **poached**  
**Reasoning:** The discovery was that the sitcom stole material.  *Filch* means **to steal secretly or casually**【293941406963917†L88-L92】, and Collins notes that to *poach* ideas is to **dishonestly or illegally use someone else’s ideas**【412283319901958†L231-L247】.  Both describe plagiarizing storylines.  *Amalgamated* and *combined* would suggest merging ideas legitimately, not theft; *indulged* and *assumed* do not relate to stealing.

## Question 58
**Prompt:** *Romantic comedies of the 1950s were characterized more by sexual \_\_\_\_ than the straightforward vulgarity that characterizes dialogue in today’s “rom‑coms.”*  
**Choices:** conversation, blatancy, **insinuation**, illusion, **innuendo**, mockery  
**Correct pair:** **insinuation** and **innuendo**  
**Reasoning:** The contrast is between subtle suggestion and overt crudeness.  *Insinuation* is **an indirect or covert suggestion or hint, especially of a derogatory nature**【727892502052470†L243-L247】, and *innuendo* is **an oblique allusion or hint—especially a veiled or equivocal reflection**【556662596551589†L93-L99】.  Both refer to subtle, suggestive hints, fitting the idea of older films implying sexual content rather than stating it bluntly.  Conversation and illusion are neutral; blatancy and mockery denote the opposite of subtle suggestion.

## Question 59
**Prompt:** *Inflation isn’t dead, only \_\_\_\_ ; as the economy turns around, the purchasing power of the dollar is likely to fall again.*  
**Choices:** paralyzed, **dormant**, indigent, itinerant, problematic, **latent**  
**Correct pair:** **dormant** and **latent**  
**Reasoning:** The statement suggests that inflation is not gone but temporarily inactive.  *Dormant* means **lying asleep or inactive; in a state of rest or inactivity**【555970730585356†L31-L44】.  *Latent* means **present but not visible or active; existing as potential**【344514688152164†L31-L33】.  Both imply inflation is temporarily inactive and could revive.  Paralyzed would suggest permanent incapacity; indigent refers to poverty; itinerant means traveling; problematic does not fit the idea of temporary inactivity.

## Question 60
**Prompt:** *Many people assume that creative work is less \_\_\_\_ than manual labor, but they underestimate the difficulty of being entirely self‑motivated.*  
**Choices:** inventive, collaborative, serious, **arduous**, **taxing**, grave  
**Correct pair:** **arduous** and **taxing**  
**Reasoning:** The sentence contrasts creative work with manual labor, implying that people think it is easier but it is in fact challenging.  *Arduous* is defined as **hard to accomplish or achieve; marked by great labor or effort**【537846987052400†L92-L104】.  *Taxing* describes a task or problem that **requires a lot of mental or physical effort**【848844182735111†L209-L213】.  Both convey that creative work can be demanding.  Other options (inventive, collaborative, serious, grave) do not connote difficulty and therefore do not correct the misconception described.

## Summary
These explanations show how understanding subtle shades of meaning helps identify the two answer choices that yield equivalent sentences.  Dictionary definitions clarify why the correct pairs fit the logical context and why the other options do not.
