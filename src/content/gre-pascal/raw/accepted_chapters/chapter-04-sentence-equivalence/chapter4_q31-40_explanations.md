# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence (Questions 31‑40)

## Question 31
* **Prompt:**  The sentence notes that leaders “failed in their search for commonalities” and concludes that they remain a very ___ group.  Because their commonalities are lacking, the blank must describe a group made up of very different members.
* **Reasoning:**  **Heterogeneous** means composed of unrelated or differing parts or elements【192019979838016†L253-L254】.  **Disparate** describes things that are essentially not alike or are distinct in kind【184179365167923†L246-L247】.  Both words convey the idea of diversity rather than similarity, which fits a group that shares no common traits.
* **Answer:** **heterogeneous**, **disparate**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Idiosyncratic* describes something peculiar to an individual rather than varied across a group.  *Unorthodox* means unconventional, and *ineffective* describes poor performance; none capture the idea of diversity.  *Inconsequential* implies trivial, not varied.

## Question 32
* **Prompt:**  A theory attempts to integrate the seemingly ___ elements of a field.  The blank should refer to elements that are distinct or unrelated but need to be brought together.
* **Reasoning:**  **Discrete** means that ideas or things are separate and distinct from each other【285068051259351†L213-L216】.  **Disparate** likewise means essentially different or distinct in kind【184179365167923†L246-L247】.  Integrating such elements is exactly what a unifying theory tries to do.
* **Answer:** **discrete**, **disparate**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Irrelevant* and *sporadic* mean unrelated or occasional but do not emphasize separateness; *undiscovered* refers to being unknown.  *Pertinent* is the opposite of irrelevant and therefore wrong.

## Question 33
* **Prompt:**  The former executive keeps a low profile because of his ___ exit from the company.  The blank must describe an exit that was improper or in bad taste, which would make someone avoid attention.
* **Reasoning:**  **Indecorous** means not decorous; it lacks decorum or propriety and is therefore unseemly【875592352778948†L209-L211】.  **Unseemly** similarly means not according with established standards of good form or taste【119583616463553†L92-L98】.  An indecorous, unseemly departure would indeed lead to shame and a low profile.
* **Answer:** **indecorous**, **unseemly**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Celebrated* and *long‑awaited* imply positive anticipation; *mysterious* and *fortuitous* describe unknown or fortunate circumstances.  None of these connote disgrace.

## Question 34
* **Prompt:**  The sentence contrasts two etymologies: one is obscure, while the latter is ___, so drawing connections is unwarranted.  The blank should mean “obvious or open to view.”
* **Reasoning:**  **Transparent**, when used in the sense of “obvious,” means clear and easy to understand or recognize【821448176571756†L124-L128】.  **Overt** means open to view or manifest【342133936952660†L83-L89】.  If one derivation is transparent and overt, it is readily apparent, so it does not support a connection to an obscure origin.
* **Answer:** **transparent**, **overt**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Complicated* and *abstruse* both mean difficult to understand—the opposite of the required meaning.  *Erudite* and *scholarly* describe learnedness, and *complicated* does not indicate openness.

## Question 35
* **Prompt:**  The prime minister’s austerity measures were criticized as being unduly ___.  The blank should mean excessively harsh or burdensome.
* **Reasoning:**  **Arduous** describes something that is difficult and tiring and involves a lot of effort【952260854395107†L207-L213】.  **Onerous** means involving or constituting a burden or being troublesome【686090074800640†L88-L94】.  These words convey that the measures demanded significant hardship, making “unduly arduous/onerous” a fitting description.
* **Answer:** **arduous**, **onerous**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Commercial* and *venal* refer to commerce or corruption, not difficulty.  *Unprincipled* and *corrupted* describe ethical failings, and *unprincipled* mislabels the measures rather than their effect.

## Question 36
* **Prompt:**  The remote spa allows visitors to unwind in a supremely ___ environment.  The blank should evoke calmness and relaxation.
* **Reasoning:**  To **appease** someone is to bring them to a state of peace, quiet, calm or contentment【199762671571418†L33-L35】.  **Soothing** means tending to soothe or having a sedative effect【289591277744677†L83-L86】.  A remote spa that appeases and soothes guests would indeed be tranquil and relaxing.
* **Answer:** **appeasing**, **soothing**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Effusive* and *blithe* suggest exuberance or cheerfulness rather than calm.  *Elegant* and *luxurious* describe style rather than serenity.

## Question 37
* **Prompt:**  A group of children attempt to cook brunch, producing soggy French toast, lukewarm coffee and a syrup fight; the result was ___.  The blank should mean laughably absurd.
* **Reasoning:**  **Farcical** refers to something ludicrous or laughably inept【107188605312867†L78-L86】.  **Comical** means funny in a strange or silly way【421739014314287†L59-L61】.  The chaotic brunch with mishaps and a syrup battle is described best as farcical and comical.
* **Answer:** **farcical**, **comical**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Convivial*, *amiable* and *gregarious* relate to friendliness and socializing rather than absurdity.  *Labile* means unstable, which doesn’t capture humor.

## Question 38
* **Prompt:**  Many websites have privacy policies that are virtually unreadable because they are written by lawyers in abstruse language; the policies are ___.
* **Reasoning:**  **Inscrutable** means not readily investigated, interpreted or understood; mysterious【930058334020832†L86-L88】.  **Unreadable** can mean extraordinarily difficult to read or comprehend; obscure【780279391034677†L28-L35】.  These words capture the impenetrable nature of legal privacy policies.
* **Answer:** **inscrutable**, **unreadable**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *A decipherable* and *a lucid* would describe clear writing, the opposite of the complaint.  *A repetitive* and *a sanctioned* do not address the difficulty of comprehension.

## Question 39
* **Prompt:**  “___ in scandal, the company could regain favor with customers only through mass firings of guilty executives.”  The blank must indicate being stuck in a difficult situation.
* **Reasoning:**  To **bog down** something is to cause it to sink in wet ground, or figuratively to become stuck【633032895840785†L66-L84】.  To **mire** someone or something is to cause it to sink in wet earth or to become trapped in a difficult situation【486381747613368†L161-L166】.  A company embroiled in scandal is metaphorically bogged down or mired; the solution—mass firings—is a drastic attempt to free itself.
* **Answer:** **bogged down**, **mired**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Rejoicing* and *glorifying* connote celebration, not trouble.  *Stoic* and *brave* describe personal qualities rather than being stuck.

## Question 40
* **Prompt:**  By framing a new law as a safety issue, the government bypassed normal legislative channels and effectively ___ all formal dissent, reducing opponents to powerless bystanders.  The blank needs a verb that means to weaken or limit opposition.
* **Reasoning:**  To **curtail** something is to reduce or limit it【179178600902307†L205-L209】.  To **undermine** means to subvert or weaken insidiously or to weaken or ruin by degrees【568315362903337†L90-L101】.  By avoiding standard processes, the government curtailed or undermined dissent, rendering opposition ineffective.
* **Answer:** **curtailing**, **undermining**.
* **Why others are wrong:**  *Targeting* and *lobbying* describe attacking or influencing rather than limiting.  *Instigating* means starting dissent, not diminishing it, and *facilitating* implies encouraging, not suppressing.
