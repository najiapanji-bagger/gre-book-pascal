# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence Questions 91‑100

The final set of Chapter 4 problems asks you to select two answer choices that produce equivalent meanings in the blank.  Each explanation below summarizes the sentence’s context, identifies the correct pair, defines the key words, and explains why the remaining options are inappropriate.  Because the GRE relies on shades of meaning, careful attention to dictionary definitions is essential.

## Q91

**Prompt.** “Foamy” viruses cause cells cultured in laboratories to swell but produce no such ___ in cells of living organisms.  The blank must refer to swelling or enlargement.

**Correct pair: *distension* and *expansion*.** In this context, *distension* means abnormal swelling of a person’s or animal’s body【154694674537260†L213-L218】, while *expansion* denotes an increase in size or volume【162322263044646†L61-L64】.  Together they describe the swelling observed in cell cultures.  None of the other choices refer to enlargement.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **distension** | abnormal swelling or stretching【154694674537260†L213-L218】 |
| **expansion** | increase in size, number or importance【162322263044646†L61-L64】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **compression** – means pressing something together, the opposite of swelling.  
* **disintegration** – refers to breaking apart or falling to pieces, not swelling.  
* **deflation** – implies shrinking or reducing size (e.g., deflating a balloon).  
* **dissolution** – describes dissolving or disbanding, which is unrelated to physical swelling.  
Thus, only *distension* and *expansion* capture the idea of cells swelling.

## Q92

**Prompt.** The eager members of the audience found the lecture topic interesting, but unfortunately the lecturer’s droning voice had a ___ effect.  The blank must convey that his monotone made listeners sleepy.

**Correct pair: *soporific* and *sleep‑inducing*.** *Soporific* means “causing or tending to cause sleep”【605986219187811†L94-L97】.  The phrase *sleep‑inducing* literally means causing sleep, so it is essentially the same as *soporific*.  The other choices do not suggest drowsiness.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **soporific** | causing or tending to cause sleep【605986219187811†L94-L97】 |
| **sleep‑inducing** | causing sleep (self‑explanatory synonym of *soporific*) |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **rousing** and **stimulating** – both imply energizing or exciting, the opposite of making listeners sleepy.  
* **bland** and **insipid** – describe something dull or lacking flavor, but they don’t specifically suggest sleepiness.  
Thus, only *soporific* and *sleep‑inducing* convey the effect of the droning voice.

## Q93

**Prompt.** Ironically, an affluent society that purchases much more food than it actually needs suffers because of that ___, since in conditions of affluence diseases related to overeating and poor nutrition seem to thrive.  The blank refers to an excess of food.

**Correct pair: *overabundance* and *superfluity*.** *Overabundance* is defined as an excessive surplus【962613769478520†L82-L83】, and *superfluity* means an excess or oversupply of something unnecessary【82999741711634†L82-L90】.  In context, both words describe having far more food than is needed.  

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **overabundance** | a large surplus; excess【962613769478520†L82-L83】 |
| **superfluity** | an oversupply or something unnecessary【82999741711634†L82-L90】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **corpulence** and **beefiness** – relate to body fat or muscularity; they do not describe an excess of food itself.  
* **commonness** and **frequency** – indicate how often something occurs, not how much of it exists.  
Therefore, only *overabundance* and *superfluity* accurately capture the notion of too much food.

## Q94

**Prompt.** Dangerously high winds ___ attempts to begin the space shuttle mission on schedule, delaying the launch by nearly a week.  The blank must mean hindered or stopped the attempts.

**Correct pair: *thwarted* and *impeded*.** To *thwart* something is to stop it from happening【222591410349947†L63-L66】, and to *impede* is to hinder or obstruct progress【876541488756569†L33-L36】.  High winds prevented the launch, so these synonyms fit perfectly.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **thwart** | to stop something from happening or someone from doing something【222591410349947†L63-L66】 |
| **impede** | to retard progress or obstruct【876541488756569†L33-L36】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **necessitated** – means made something necessary; high winds did not make the launch necessary.  
* **forfeited** and **sacrificed** – imply giving up something voluntarily; the delay wasn’t a voluntary sacrifice.  
* **imposed** – means forced; high winds did not impose an action on someone, they hindered it.  
Thus, only *thwarted* and *impeded* properly capture the delays caused by weather.

## Q95

**Prompt.** The guest speaker on Oprah Winfrey’s talk show offended the audience by first ___ them and then refusing to moderate these intemperate remarks.  The blank requires a verb meaning “to scold or lecture harshly.”

**Correct pair: *haranguing* and *berating*.** To *harangue* is to deliver a ranting speech or tirade【652431949362963†L91-L104】, while to *berate* is to scold vehemently and at length【349895916906282†L93-L96】.  Both words denote harsh lecturing, which offended the audience.  

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **harangue** | a ranting speech or lecture delivered to an audience【652431949362963†L91-L104】 |
| **berate** | to scold or condemn vehemently and at length【349895916906282†L93-L96】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **flattering** and **commending** – involve praise; they are the opposite of attacking the audience.  
* **enchanting** and **enthralling** – refer to delighting or captivating listeners, not insulting them.  
Thus, only *haranguing* and *berating* fit the context of offensively lecturing the audience.

## Q96

**Prompt.** Actors in melodramas often emphasized tense moments by being ___; for example, raising their voices and pretending to swoon.  The blank describes exaggeratedly emotional performance.

**Correct pair: *histrionic* and *theatrical*.** *Histrionic* means deliberately affected, overly dramatic, or emotional【708335187386180†L85-L87】.  *Theatrical* likewise suggests acting that is artificial and extravagant【587782345535050†L30-L36】.  Both words match the melodramatic gestures described.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **histrionic** | deliberately affected; overly dramatic or emotional【708335187386180†L85-L87】 |
| **theatrical** | suggestive of theater; artificial or extravagantly histrionic【587782345535050†L30-L36】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **imperious** – describes someone overbearing or commanding, not theatrical.  
* **inscrutable** – means difficult to understand or interpret.  
* **convivial** and **gregarious** – refer to sociable, cheerful behavior; they do not imply dramatic performance.  
Thus, only *histrionic* and *theatrical* properly capture the hammy acting style.

## Q97

**Prompt.** Just as glass windows offer buildings both light and insulation, certain atmospheric gases admit incoming sunlight and ___ heat radiated from the ground, preventing warmth from escaping.  The blank must mean “to keep in” rather than letting something out.

**Correct pair: *contain* and *hold*.** To *contain* is to keep something harmful within limits and not allow it to spread【785755435445746†L110-L117】.  To *hold* means to enclose and keep something in a container or within bounds【718001562674096†L224-L229】.  Both words describe retaining heat.  

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **contain** | to keep something within limits, preventing it from escaping【785755435445746†L110-L117】 |
| **hold** | to enclose and keep in a container or within bounds【718001562674096†L224-L229】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **release**, **dispense**, **pass on**, and **transmit** – all imply letting something go or sending it elsewhere, the opposite of trapping heat.  
Thus, only *contain* and *hold* convey the greenhouse effect of retaining heat.

## Q98

**Prompt.** A judicious biography must be a ___ representation that depicts both the strengths and the weaknesses of the subject, avoiding the two extremes of eulogy and indictment.  The blank must refer to fairness and balance.

**Correct pair: *equitable* and *evenhanded*.** *Equitable* means fair and impartial【9163466327571†L86-L88】.  *Even‑handed* similarly refers to treating everyone fairly and equally【384841994763608†L60-L65】.  A judicious biography should not be biased toward praise or blame, so these terms fit perfectly.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **equitable** | fair; dealing equally with all concerned【9163466327571†L86-L88】 |
| **even‑handed** | treating everyone fairly and equally【384841994763608†L60-L65】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **polarized** – means divided into two opposite extremes, the very bias the sentence says to avoid.  
* **holistic** and **integrated** – refer to considering the whole; they do not necessarily imply fairness or impartiality.  
* **imaginative** – suggests creativity, not objectivity.  
Thus, only *equitable* and *even‑handed* capture the balanced tone required.

## Q99

**Prompt.** For a long time, most doctors maintained that taking massive doses of vitamins was relatively harmless; now, however, some are warning that excessive dosage can be ___.  The blank must suggest danger, even death.

**Correct pair: *lethal* and *toxic*.** *Lethal* means capable of causing death【210703698041139†L61-L64】, and *toxic* means poisonous or harmful to living organisms【989572087533298†L61-L64】.  Excessive vitamin doses can thus be deadly or poisonous.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **lethal** | able to cause or causing death【210703698041139†L61-L64】 |
| **toxic** | poisonous; harmful to living organisms【989572087533298†L61-L64】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **wasteful** – describes inefficient use of resources, which doesn’t address harm.  
* **salubrious** and **wholesome** – mean health‑giving; these contradict the warning.  
* **inane** – means silly or pointless.  
Therefore, *lethal* and *toxic* are the only choices that capture the potential danger of excessive vitamins.

## Q100

**Prompt.** The spotted bowerbird has a ___ for amassing the bright shiny objects it needs for decorating its bower; it will enter houses to pilfer cutlery, coins, thimbles, nails, screws, and even car keys.  The blank describes the bird’s strong tendency or skill.

**Correct pair: *knack* and *penchant*.** A *knack* is a skill or ability to do something easily and well【364601489123645†L58-L63】.  A *penchant* is a strong and continued inclination or liking【446305875517604†L89-L92】.  These terms capture the bird’s talent and inclination for collecting shiny objects.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **knack** | a skill or ability to do something easily and well【364601489123645†L58-L63】 |
| **penchant** | a strong and continued inclination; strong liking【446305875517604†L89-L92】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**  
* **aversion** and **repugnance** – mean dislike or disgust, which is opposite of the bird’s liking for shiny things.  
* **indifference** and **apathy** – imply lack of interest; the bird clearly shows enthusiasm.  
Thus, only *knack* and *penchant* align with the bird’s behavior.
