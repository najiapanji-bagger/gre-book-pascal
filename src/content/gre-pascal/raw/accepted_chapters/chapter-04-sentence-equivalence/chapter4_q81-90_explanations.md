# Chapter 4 – Sentence Equivalence Questions 81‑90

This section continues the step‑by‑step analysis of the GRE Pascal book’s Sentence Equivalence exercises. For questions 81–90 you must select **two** answer choices that produce the same meaning in the blank. For each problem below, I paraphrase the clue, identify the correct pair, show a table that defines the key words, and briefly explain why the remaining choices do not work.

## Q81

**Prompt.** *Some people believe that private philanthropy alone can address poverty in developing nations, but in fact organizations such as UNICEF and WHO, alongside local governments, are _____ in this process.* The sentence emphasizes that these public institutions play a vital part in alleviating poverty, not a minor role.

**Correct pair: *instrumental in* and *invaluable to*.** Both options convey indispensability. If someone or something is **instrumental in** a process, it means they are one of the most important influences causing it to happen【391946056960835†L63-L65】. Something **invaluable** is extremely useful or indispensable【710991460258297†L61-L66】. Thus the sentence asserts that international and local institutions are essential in combating poverty. Other choices such as *incidental* or *peripheral* suggest unimportance; *negligible* means too small to matter; and *obsolete* implies that the institutions are outdated, all of which contradict the intended emphasis on significance.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **instrumental (in)** | playing a crucial or very important part in achieving something【391946056960835†L63-L65】 |
| **invaluable (to)** | extremely useful or indispensable【710991460258297†L61-L66】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **incidental / peripheral** – these imply something is minor or secondary to the main process; the sentence stresses that the organizations are central, not incidental.
* **negligible** – means so small or unimportant as to be not worth considering; it contradicts the idea that international agencies have an essential role.
* **obsolete** – refers to something outdated or no longer useful; this is irrelevant when describing organizations that remain active and important.
* **dissuading** (if offered) – means discouraging someone from acting; nothing in the sentence implies that the organizations prevent others from helping.

## Q82

**Prompt.** *In one instance illustrating Prince Metternich’s obsession with collecting books, he employed several naval captains to purchase rare works abroad, eventually adding an entire Oriental library to his _____ collection.* The context shows that his personal library was rapidly growing as he kept buying more volumes.

**Correct pair: *burgeoning* and *proliferating*.** The appropriate pair suggests rapid expansion. If a collection is **burgeoning**, it is growing or developing quickly【593266936453433†L211-L214】. To **proliferate** means to increase a lot and suddenly in number【714269554517400†L61-L64】. Hence Metternich’s library was expanding dramatically. Choices like *waning* or *subsiding* signal decline; *homogeneous* implies uniformity rather than growth; and *motley* means varied or heterogeneous, which does not fit the idea of size or rate of growth.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **burgeoning** | rapidly growing or developing; flourishing【593266936453433†L211-L214】 |
| **proliferating** | increasing greatly in number or amount【714269554517400†L61-L64】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **waning / subsiding** – both denote decreasing or diminishing; they contradict the evidence that his library was expanding.
* **homogeneous** – means of the same kind or nature and relates to uniformity, not size or growth; it fails to describe a rapidly enlarging collection.
* **motley** – refers to being variegated or composed of diverse elements; while his library could have been diverse, the emphasis in the sentence is on quantity, not variety.
* **pallid** (if present) – means lacking in vitality or color, which has nothing to do with accumulation of volumes.

## Q83

**Prompt.** *Francis discovered that by _____ his anger and resentment—thus avoiding open confrontation—he could deal with opponents more successfully than those who openly defied their adversaries.* The clue implies Francis suppressed negative emotions instead of venting them.

**Correct pair: *stifling* and *suppressing*.** Both words describe deliberately holding back or quelling an emotion. To **stifle** something is to cut it off, hold it back or smother it【547193474624345†L20-L22】. To **suppress** means to prevent something from being expressed or known【407282314694807†L96-L104】. By stifling/suppressing his anger, Francis avoided conflict. Other choices such as *stimulating*, *stirring up* or *rousing* all mean encouraging or provoking emotions, which is the opposite of what the sentence describes. *Expressing* means to convey or reveal emotions rather than conceal them.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **stifling** | cutting off or holding back something, such as anger or resentment【547193474624345†L20-L22】 |
| **suppressing** | preventing something from being expressed or known【407282314694807†L96-L104】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **expressing** – means conveying or communicating feelings; this would intensify conflict rather than avoid it.
* **rousing / stirring up / stimulating** – each means to incite or excite emotions; again, these would exacerbate tensions, contrary to Francis’s strategy of quelling his anger.
* **exhibiting** (if offered) – indicates showing or displaying, not suppressing, feelings.

## Q84

**Prompt.** *A recent book by two literary critics presents an excess of detail, providing far more information than one can readily digest.*

**Correct pair: *a surfeit* and *an abundance*.** Here the blank describes how much detail the book contains. A **surfeit** is an amount that is too large or in excess【805957375350536†L211-L217】. An **abundance** likewise means a very large quantity or more than enough【121957136838656†L61-L68】. Both choices match the sense of “too much” detail. By contrast, *a modicum* or *a paucity* indicate a small amount; *a dearth* and *a deficiency* refer to a lack; and *a paucity* specifically implies scarcity.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **surfeit** | an excessive or too‑great amount【805957375350536†L211-L217】 |
| **abundance** | a very large quantity; more than enough of something【121957136838656†L61-L68】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **a modicum / a paucity / a dearth / a deficiency** – each of these refers to a small or insufficient quantity; they fail to describe the text’s excessive detail.
* **a paucity** – specifically implies scarcity, again contrary to the phrase “far more information than one can digest.”
* **a smattering** (if present) – means a slight or superficial amount, not an overload.

## Q85

**Prompt.** *Despite global efforts to eradicate malaria, this mosquito‑borne disease continues to _____ : the World Health Organization estimates that it still affects up to 500 million people a year.* The sentence implies that malaria persists and even thrives.

**Correct pair: *thrive* and *flourish*.** Both words convey vigorous growth or prosperity. To **thrive** is “to grow vigorously”【989698436340399†L87-L90】. To **flourish** means “to grow luxuriantly; thrive”【895386400899470†L96-L100】. Therefore the disease continues to thrive/flourish despite efforts to eradicate it. Options such as *abate*, *subside*, *relinquish* or *surrender* suggest reduction or giving up, which conflicts with the observation that malaria remains widespread.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **thrive** | to grow vigorously; prosper【989698436340399†L87-L90】 |
| **flourish** | to grow luxuriantly; thrive【895386400899470†L96-L100】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **abate / subside** – both indicate lessening in intensity or severity; they would fit only if malaria were diminishing, which it is not.
* **relinquish / surrender** – mean to give up or let go; these verbs cannot modify a disease.
* **diminish** (if present) – similarly implies decline rather than persistence.

## Q86

**Prompt.** *Exposure to prolonged noise impairs blood‑pressure regulation in humans, yet some experimental results seem to _____ the proposed correlation.* The blank signals that the findings make the relationship unclear rather than support it.

**Correct pair: *muddy* and *obscure*.** These verbs indicate making something unclear. To **muddy** an issue is to confuse it or make it harder to understand【874980521490041†L155-L167】. To **obscure** means to prevent something from being seen or heard or to make it difficult to discover or understand【415414089319935†L142-L165】. Thus, the results muddy/obscure the correlation. By contrast, *buttress* and *reinforce* would strengthen a hypothesis; *conflate* or *blend* suggest combining things; and *clarify* would make things clearer, the opposite of what happens here.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **muddy** | to make something confusing, cloudy or unclear【874980521490041†L155-L167】 |
| **obscure** | to prevent something from being seen or understood; to make difficult to discover【415414089319935†L142-L165】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **buttress / reinforce** – these mean to strengthen or support a claim; the sentence suggests the results do not support but rather cloud the correlation.
* **conflate / blend** – refer to combining two things; they do not capture the idea of introducing confusion.
* **clarify** – to make clear; using this would contradict the statement that the results make the relationship murkier.

## Q87

**Prompt.** *Farming had been profitable on the Great Plains for many decades, but by 1938 agricultural practices and years of inadequate precipitation had denuded the land. The practices were thus _____.* This sentence implies that the farming methods were careless and ultimately damaging.

**Correct pair: *imprudent* and *reckless*.** Both adjectives describe acting without sufficient thought for the consequences. Being **imprudent** means acting unwisely without considering likely results【903238571321844†L61-L65】. **Reckless** behavior involves acting without regard for danger or the effects on others【520647830416943†L213-L215】. Hence, the farmers’ imprudent/reckless practices led to the ecological disaster. Other choices such as *shrewd* or *innovative* are positive; *conscientious* implies carefulness; and *pioneering* means original or ground‑breaking—all inconsistent with the implied negligence.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **imprudent** | unwise; failing to consider likely results【903238571321844†L61-L65】 |
| **reckless** | acting without care about danger or consequences【520647830416943†L213-L215】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **shrewd / conscientious** – describe someone perceptive or careful; the sentence criticizes the farmers for lacking foresight, so these words do not fit.
* **innovative / pioneering** – suggest creativity and originality; the Great Plains farmers were not applauded for inventiveness but blamed for harmful practices.
* **meticulous** (if present) – implies great attention to detail; again, it contradicts the notion of carelessness.

## Q88

**Prompt.** *Cathedrals typically take decades or even centuries to build, so no one expected the National Cathedral to be completed with _____.* Because long construction times are the norm, finishing a cathedral quickly would be remarkable.

**Correct pair: *dispatch* and *promptness*.** These nouns both refer to speed or swift action. **Dispatch**, as a noun, means promptness or speed【960295245479011†L50-L58】. **Promptness** conveys the quality of acting without delay or being quick to do something【216151138916335†L218-L223】. Thus finishing the National Cathedral with dispatch/promptness would be surprising. Other options like *torpor* or *inertia* indicate sluggishness; *durability* and *resilience* refer to lasting strength rather than speed; and *resistance* or *inertia* would imply opposition or stagnation.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **dispatch** | prompt or speedy transaction; promptness or speed【960295245479011†L50-L58】 |
| **promptness** | acting without delay; quickness or readiness to act【216151138916335†L218-L223】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **torpor / inertia** – both denote sluggishness or inactivity; they do not describe rapid completion.
* **durability / resilience** – refer to the ability to withstand wear or recover quickly; these relate to longevity or toughness, not speed.
* **inspiration** (if present) – refers to creative motivation rather than quick construction.

## Q89

**Prompt.** *Although the late Supreme Court Justice Thurgood Marshall stipulated that his papers be available only to scholars, the Library of Congress _____ his wishes and exhibited the documents to the general public.* The library did not follow his instructions.

**Correct pair: *disregarded* and *ignored*.** Both verbs mean paying no attention to something. To **disregard** something is to ignore it or treat it as unimportant【621211161699220†L59-L108】. **Ignored** has the same sense of deliberately paying no heed. The Library of Congress disregarded/ignored Marshall’s wishes and displayed his papers anyway. Other options such as *heeded*, *honored* or *executed* describe following instructions; these are antonyms in this context. *Accomplished* means achieved; *observed* means obeyed; none of these match the idea of flouting his stipulation.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **disregarded** | ignored; paid no attention to【621211161699220†L59-L108】 |
| **ignored** | took no notice of; refused to acknowledge【621211161699220†L59-L108】 (synonym) |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **heeded / honored / executed / observed** – all imply complying with someone’s wishes; the Library did the opposite.
* **accomplished** – means carried out or achieved; the prompt is about ignoring directions, not achieving a goal.
* **obeyed** (if listed) – similarly means to follow rules or requests, which contradicts the library’s actions.

## Q90

**Prompt.** *The announcement that the city would raze the landmark school building was such a distressing _____ that it provoked an outcry.* The blank refers to the upsetting announcement itself.

**Correct pair: *disclosure* and *revelation*.** These nouns both describe making something known, especially information that was previously hidden or unexpected. A **disclosure** is the act of making something known【932929123785841†L60-L64】 or the thing revealed itself【932929123785841†L118-L124】. A **revelation** similarly refers to a surprising fact or the act of revealing something previously unknown【556836258369451†L48-L60】. The city’s decision to demolish the school was a distressing disclosure/revelation that shocked the public. Other choices like *calamity* or *mishap* describe unfortunate events rather than announcements; *instance* and *illustration* are generic nouns that lack the connotation of unveiling information.

**Keyword meanings**

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **disclosure** | the act of making something known or the fact that is made known【932929123785841†L60-L64】【932929123785841†L118-L124】 |
| **revelation** | the act of revealing or disclosing something; a striking disclosure【556836258369451†L48-L60】 |

**Why the other options are wrong.**

* **calamity / mishap** – describe accidents or disasters, not the act of announcing something.
* **instance / illustration** – generic terms for examples; they do not capture the sense of unveiling upsetting news.
* **catastrophe** (if offered) – refers to a terrible event rather than the communication of a decision.
