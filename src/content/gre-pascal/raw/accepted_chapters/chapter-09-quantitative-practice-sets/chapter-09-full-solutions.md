# Chapter 9 – Solutions for Practice Sets 9.1–9.3

The following summaries provide answers and brief reasoning for all questions in Practice Sets 9.1 through 9.3.  When a problem depended on a diagram or the scan was unclear, a note is included.  Questions from later practice sets (9.4–9.6) were not solved in Stage 2 because the relevant pages were missing or damaged.

## Practice Set 9.1 – Part 1

1. **Midpoints:**  Segments RS and ST have lengths 6 and 9.  The midpoints divide the total interval evenly, so the distance between them equals ST.  *Answer C.*

2. **Mean vs median:**  Without specific numbers the mean could be above, below or equal to the median.  *Cannot determine (D).*  

3. **Squares of z:**  Expanding the expressions shows that the average of (z minus 1)^2 and (z plus 1)^2 exceeds z squared by 1.  *Quantity A greater (A).*  

4. **Circumference difference:**  The difference in circumferences depends only on the 12‑unit radius difference.  Using pi equals 3 yields 72.  *Answer C.*  

5. **Factor puzzle:**  Factor 2000 into prime powers.  Assign exponents so that x^3 and y^4 reproduce the factorisation; one solution is x equals 5 and y equals 4.  Their product is 20.  

6. **Height range:**  Use the top of the shortest interval and the bottom of the tallest interval.  Range equals 160 minus 144 equals 16.  

7. **Garden patio:**  With perimeter 42 and width three quarters of length, solve for dimensions (12 by 9).  Area equals 108.  

8. **Uninspected chairs:**  Chairs inspected are multiples of 3 or 5.  Numbers not divisible by 3 or 5 include 14, 74 and 94.  

9. **Triangle coordinates:**  Base length is five.  For area ten, height must be four.  Coordinates with y value four above the axis work.  

10. **Car speed:**  Convert 108 seconds to hours (0.03 h).  Divide 2.4 km by 0.03 h to get 80 km/h.  

11. **Dosage percent:**  A 120‑pound person’s typical dose is 16 cc; prescribed is 18 cc.  Difference divided by typical equals 12.5 %.  

12. **Circle ratio:**  If AB is the diameter of the small circle and the radius of the large circle, the small circle’s area is one quarter of the large.  25 %.  

## Practice Set 9.1 – Part 2

1. **Isosceles side:**  The equal sides must be the two longer sides; therefore l equals 7.  

2. **Thousands digit:**  Numbers greater than 3999 can have thousands digit equal to 3, 4 or more.  No comparison to 3 is possible.  

3. **Standard deviations:**  Both height ranges span 12 centimetres, so without distribution data the standard deviations are treated as equal.  

4. **Only one class:**  Use inclusion–exclusion; exactly 30 students are in only one course, matching the comparison number.  

5. **Solving x and y:**  Because x squared equals 16 and y squared equals 64, x and y can be positive or negative.  No fixed relationship.  

6. **Percent relation:**  0.1 percent equals one‑thousandth.  Setting 10 raised to x equal to one‑thousandth of 10 raised to y gives y = x plus 3.  

7. **Full‑time workers:**  Thirty percent of workers are full time; 60 % of these are women, so 40 % are men.  

8. **Expression value:**  Plugging the defined numbers into the expression simplifies to 4.  

9. **Sequence terms:**  Working backward from the fourth term (19) shows the sequence begins at 5 and continues 5, 7, 11, 19, 35, 67.  Terms include 5, 11, 35 and 67.  

10. **Solving inequalities:**  The solution set for both inequalities is between −8.33 and −6.8.  Only −7.5 falls in this interval.  

11. **Functions equality:**  Solve |2n + 1| = 3; the smallest integer solution is −2.  

12. **Decimal product:**  If x equals 0.1 then 1 plus 1/x times 1/x equals 11 times 10 equals 110.  

13. **Salary ratio:**  From the equal final salaries, Anne’s initial salary was approximately 15.8 % higher than Jack’s.  

14. **Revenue growth:**  Over three years the revenue grew from 4.50 to 5.75 billion.  The average annual increase is roughly 9 %.  

15. **Other greeting cards:**  Cards sold in 2021 total 4.6 billion.  Subtract the 3.9 billion listed to find about 0.7 billion cards for other occasions.  

## Practice Set 9.2 – Part 1

1. **Circle area:**  A circumference of 18π yields radius 9 and area 81π (about 254), which is greater than 27.  

2. **Fraction comparison:**  Evaluating the given sum yields 84 divided by 4 equals 21.  Both quantities are equal.  

3. **Similar triangles:**  The right‑triangle altitude splits the triangle into similar triangles; the labelled acute angles are congruent.  Quantities are equal.  

4. **Mean vs median (company X):**  Average salary is about 37,200 dollars; median is below 37,000 because at least 11 salaries are below this value.  The mean is larger.  

5. **Integer relation:**  Write 8^m as 2^(3m) and 16^t as 2^(4t).  Equate exponents: 3m = 4t so m = 4t/3.  

6. **Hypotenuse from area:**  With leg ratio 2:5 and area 20, the legs are 4 and 10; the hypotenuse is 2√29.  

7. **Applicants percentage:**  Nine applicants have neither work experience nor a degree.  Among 36 without experience, this is 25 %.  

8. **Restaurant profit:**  Profit lies between 2 % and 22 % of revenue.  For 80,000 dollars, profits of 1,800, 10,000 and 16,000 dollars fall in this interval.  

9. **Water tank:**  In eight hours a tank leaking 0.5 ounce per second loses 14,400 ounces.  Acceptable initial amounts must be less than or equal to 14,400 and multiples of 0.5.  

10. **Quadratic expression:**  The solutions to x^2 − 5x + 4 = 0 are 1 and 4.  Substituting into the given expression yields approximately 2.5; the answer key lists 2.9, so this question requires review.  

11. **Rectangle triangle:**  Extending AB so that BE equals AD makes triangle CDE half the rectangle area.  The rectangle’s area is 42; thus the triangle’s area is 21.  

12. **Seating arrangements:**  Fix Martha in the centre; arrange the other four in 24 ways.  

## Practice Set 9.2 – Part 2

1. **Comparing products:**  Expanding both products shows they simplify to the same expression.  The quantities are equal.  

2. **Powers of −1:**  Consecutive integers squared plus the next integer always produce an odd exponent for −1.  The resulting expression yields 3 versus 0; the first is larger.  

3. **Marble probability:**  The probability of drawing one red and one blue from a jar of ten red and ten blue marbles is 10/19, which is greater than one half.  

4. **Triangle areas:**  A right isosceles triangle with legs two has area 2, larger than the area of an equilateral triangle with side two (about 1.732).  

5. **Compound interest:**  Without knowing the relationship between X and Y, the interest earned on the deposits cannot be compared.  

6. **Minimum average:**  To minimise the average of 21 integers with a median of 10 and largest value 16, assign as many low values as allowed while maintaining the median.  The minimum average is 6.  

7. **Dividing primes:**  After cancelling factors of 3 and 13 from the fraction, the only y value that produces an integer numerator is 143.  

8. **Line without integer points:**  The line y = x + 1/2 never passes through a point with both coordinates integers.  

9. **Dot counts:**  Given the dot ratio (two black and one green for every four red), the total number of dots must be a multiple of seven.  84 and 224 satisfy this; other options are dubious because of scanning errors.  

10. **Class average:**  If there are more than three times as many boys as girls with averages of 78 and 94, the class average is between 78 and about 82.  Values 78, 80 and 82 are possible.  

11. **Exponential equation:**  Rearranging the equation shows that x equals −1 and y equals −1.  

12. **Sale versus regular price:**  A 20 % discount means the sale price is 80 % of the regular price.  The regular price is 25 % greater than the sale price.  

13. **Inventory percent change:**  Successive percentage changes yield a June value for business K of about 28,500 dollars, a May‑to‑June ratio at M of about 89 %, and the largest total change from April to June occurs for business V.  

## Practice Set 9.3 – Part 1

1. **Compare y with −5:**  Any real y less than −6 is smaller than −5.  

2. **Median versus 25:**  The median of the set {23, 24, 26, 27, x} depends on x; without specifying x, no comparison is possible.  

3. **Slope comparison:**  The slope of the line through (0,a) and (3,a−8) is −8/3, which is greater than −17/6.  

4. **Prime factor exponents:**  In the factorisation of 51, both the exponents of 2 and 5 are zero.  

5. **Book club meetings:**  Writing equations for meetings gives four novels and eight non‑fiction books.  

6. **Geometry with BCDE:**  The necessary diagram was not available; this question remains unsolved.  

7. **Sum of integers:**  Summing all integers strictly between −26 and 24 yields −2.  

8. **Playlist averages:**  At least seven songs must be shorter than 3.6 minutes, and at least seven must be longer than 3.4 minutes; no song must equal the mean.

## Practice Set 9.3 – Part 2

1. **Units digit of 24^x:**  The units digit cycles through 4 and 6, giving two distinct values.  The question is ambiguous and is flagged.  

2. **Line at 40 degrees:**  Without the diagram, we cannot compare the coordinates of P; this question is flagged.  

3. **Median versus range:**  Without knowing the number of elements in S, the comparison cannot be made; flagged.  

4. **Square root vs square:**  For numbers between zero and one, the square root is always greater than the square.  

5. **Minimise deviation:**  In the set {6, 8, 12, 14, x}, choose x = 10 to minimise the standard deviation.  

6. **Science club membership:**  In a 100‑student model, 20 are in the science club and 30 are in the band; 25 are in band only, leaving 5 in both.  Thus 15 of 20 science‑club members are not in the band (75 %).  

7. **Intersecting lines:**  Among the given lines, y = 2x − 2 intersects the V‑shaped graph of y = |2x| + 4.  

8. **Race times:**  Race times for speeds between 4 mph and 7 mph range from about 43 to 75 minutes.  Times of 48, 60 and 75 minutes fit.  

9. **Tile area:**  A rectangle with length 4–6 inches and width 3–6 inches has an area between 12 and 36 square inches (about 1/12 to 1/4 square feet).  Areas 1/8 and 1/6 square feet fit.  

10. **Peanut butter rate:**  Combined, the machines produce 10/9 pounds per minute; one pound takes 0.9 minutes or 54 seconds.  The official answer lists 40 seconds, so this question is flagged.  

11. **Percent comparison:**  There is disagreement between straightforward calculation (about 9.87) and the official answer (5.17); flagged.  

12. **Prime factor sum:**  The exponent sum of the prime factorisation of 8! is 11.  The official answer lists 8; flagged.  

13. **Bird sightings:**  Using the table, January accounts for one third of combined winter sightings for three species; the species with the smallest spread across seasons has the lowest standard deviation; the wren’s sightings increase by about 20 % from spring to summer.
