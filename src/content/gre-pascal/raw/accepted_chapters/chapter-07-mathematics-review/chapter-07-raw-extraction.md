# Chapter 7 Raw Extraction

This document contains the raw text and notes extracted from the scanned pages of Chapter 7.  The information is grouped by section and follows the order of the pages.  Each entry lists the source page number and the path to the corresponding image file in the archive.  Items that were illegible or unclear are noted for review.

## 7.1 Algebra and Arithmetic (pages 143–144)

- **Number concepts and notation** – The opening table on page 143 introduces the set of real numbers, natural numbers, integers and rational numbers.  It explains how to locate numbers on the number line and defines the absolute value function.  *Source: page 143 (images/page_143.jpg)*.
- **Prime numbers and divisibility** – A short description of prime numbers (integers greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors) is given.  Divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9 are summarised.  *Source: page 143 (images/page_143.jpg)*.
- **Factors and multiples** – Definitions of factor, multiple and greatest common divisor (gcd) are provided.  The least common multiple (lcm) is defined as the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of two given numbers.  *Source: page 143 (images/page_143.jpg)*.
- **Special products** – Page 143 lists common factoring identities: the difference of squares (a² – b² = (a + b)(a – b)), the square of a sum (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² and the square of a difference (a – b)² = a² – 2ab + b².  *Source: page 143 (images/page_143.jpg)*.
- **Exponent rules** – Page 144 reviews properties of exponents: x^a × x^b = x^(a + b); (x^a)^b = x^(ab); x^a ÷ x^b = x^(a – b); and x⁰ = 1 for x ≠ 0.  It also explains fractional and negative exponents: x^(1/n) is the nth root of x, and x^(–a) = 1 / x^a.  *Source: page 144 (images/page_144.jpg)*.
- **Absolute value** – The definition |x| = x if x ≥ 0 and |x| = –x if x < 0 is given.  Properties include |x| ≥ 0, |x|^2 = x^2 and |xy| = |x|·|y|.  *Source: page 144 (images/page_144.jpg)*.
- **Order of operations** – A reminder to perform operations in the following order: parentheses, exponents, multiplication and division (from left to right), addition and subtraction (from left to right).  *Source: page 144 (images/page_144.jpg)*.

## 7.2 Geometry (pages 145–148)

- **Angles and triangles** – Page 145 lists angle types (acute, right, obtuse, straight) and the sum of angles in a triangle (180°).  It describes equilateral, isosceles and right triangles and notes that an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.  Pythagorean triples (3–4–5 and 5–12–13) and special right triangles (45‑45‑90 and 30‑60‑90) are included.  *Source: page 145 (images/page_145.jpg)*.
- **Polygons and quadrilaterals** – Definitions of polygon types and formulas for the sum of the interior angles of an n‑sided polygon, (n – 2)·180°, are given.  The properties of parallelograms, rectangles, rhombi, squares and trapezoids are summarised.  *Source: page 146 (images/page_146.jpg)*.
- **Circles** – Page 146 provides formulas for the circumference (2πr), area (πr²) and arc length of a circle.  It also defines chords, diameters, radii, tangents and central angles.  *Source: page 146 (images/page_146.jpg)*.
- **Three‑dimensional figures** – Volume and surface‑area formulas are presented for rectangular prisms, cylinders, spheres, cones and pyramids.  For example, the volume of a cylinder is πr²h and the surface area is 2πr(h + r).  *Source: page 146 (images/page_146.jpg)*.
- **Coordinate geometry** – Page 147 introduces the Cartesian plane, including definitions of the x‑ and y‑axes, quadrants and the distance between two points.  The slope of a line (rise over run), the equation of a line (y = mx + b), conditions for parallel and perpendicular lines (equal slopes or negative reciprocals) and the midpoint formula are stated.  *Source: page 147 (images/page_147.jpg)*.

## 7.3 Data Analysis (pages 148–150)

- **Descriptive statistics** – The mean (arithmetic average) is defined as the sum of a set divided by the number of terms.  The median is the middle value when the data are ordered, and the mode is the most frequently occurring value.  The range is the difference between the largest and smallest values.  Standard deviation measures the spread around the mean.  *Source: page 148 (images/page_148.jpg)*.
- **Normal distribution** – A diagram illustrates the bell‑shaped curve, noting that about 68 % of data lie within one standard deviation of the mean, 95 % within two standard deviations and 99.7 % within three standard deviations.  *Source: page 148 (images/page_148.jpg)*.
- **Counting principles** – Page 150 describes the fundamental counting principle, permutations and combinations.  The number of ways to arrange n distinct objects is n!, while the number of ways to choose k objects from n without regard to order is n choose k.  *Source: page 150 (images/page_150.jpg)*.
- **Probability** – Basic probability is defined as the number of favourable outcomes divided by the total number of equally likely outcomes.  The complement rule (P(A) = 1 – P(not A)) and the addition rule for mutually exclusive events (P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)) are provided.  *Source: page 150 (images/page_150.jpg)*.

## 7.4 Measurement and Currency (page 151)

- **Length, area and volume conversions** – Tables list common U.S. customary units: 1 foot = 12 inches; 1 yard = 3 feet; 1 mile = 5,280 feet; 1 square foot = 144 square inches; 1 square yard = 9 square feet; 1 cubic foot = 1,728 cubic inches; 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 128 fluid ounces.  *Source: page 151 (images/page_151.jpg)*.
- **Weight and mass** – 1 pound = 16 ounces; 1 ton = 2,000 pounds.  *Source: page 151 (images/page_151.jpg)*.
- **Time** – 1 hour = 60 minutes; 1 day = 24 hours; 1 week = 7 days; 1 year ≈ 52 weeks = 365 days (ignoring leap years).  *Source: page 151 (images/page_151.jpg)*.
- **U.S. currency** – A table lists coins and bills: penny (1 ¢), nickel (5 ¢), dime (10 ¢), quarter (25 ¢), half‑dollar (50 ¢), dollar bill ($1), five‑dollar bill ($5), ten‑dollar bill ($10), twenty‑dollar bill ($20), fifty‑dollar bill ($50) and hundred‑dollar bill ($100).  *Source: page 151 (images/page_151.jpg)*.
